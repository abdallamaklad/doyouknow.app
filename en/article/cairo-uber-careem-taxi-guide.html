    • Uber Entered Egypt in 2014

    Uber launched in Cairo in 2014, making Egypt one of its earliest Middle East markets outside the Gulf. It started with a small fleet of private sedans aimed at expatriates and business travelers who wanted metered, cashless rides instead of negotiating with street taxis.

    Growth was slow at first because smartphone and card penetration were limited, but adoption accelerated after 2016 as mobile data got cheaper and cash payment was added, removing the biggest barrier for local riders who did not use credit cards.

    Careem Is a Dubai-Founded Rival, Now Owned by Uber

    Careem was founded in Dubai in 2012 and entered Egypt shortly after Uber, building a strong local following by leaning into cash payments and Arabic-first design from the start. It became the default app for many Egyptians before Uber caught up on those features.

    In 2020, Uber acquired Careem for roughly $3.1 billion but kept it running as a separate app and brand. In Egypt today, both apps operate independently on the same underlying driver marketplace in many cases, which is why prices and wait times can differ trip to trip.

    Both Apps Show an Upfront Fare Before You Book

    Uber and Careem both quote a fixed price before you confirm a ride, calculated from distance, estimated time, and current demand. This upfront model removes the haggling that comes with flagging a street taxi, and the price shown is what you pay unless the route changes significantly.

    Fares are shown in Egyptian pounds and can be paid in cash to the driver or through a linked card or in-app wallet. Riders should still confirm the destination pin is accurate, since a wrong drop-off point can shift the quoted price after the trip starts.

    Cairo Traffic Makes Surge Pricing Common at Rush Hour

    Both apps use dynamic pricing that rises when demand outstrips available drivers, which happens daily in Cairo between roughly 7-10am and 4-8pm as traffic on the Ring Road and downtown bridges peaks. Fares during these windows can run noticeably higher than midday quotes.

    Rain, which is rare in Cairo but paralyzes traffic when it hits, and major events like concerts or football matches at Cairo Stadium also trigger surge pricing. Checking the fare estimate a few minutes apart, or walking a short distance from a congested pickup zone, can sometimes reduce it.

    Economy Is the Cheapest Tier on Both Apps

    Uber's entry-level tier in Egypt is called UberX, and Careem's equivalent is Careem Go, both using compact or midsize sedans. These are the cheapest options and the ones most riders default to for everyday trips across Cairo and Giza.

    Above economy sit comfort tiers with newer cars and more legroom, and XL options with larger vehicles for groups of five or six plus luggage. Business tiers with premium sedans exist mainly in central Cairo and near five-star hotels, at a noticeably higher price.

    Cairo International Airport Has Designated Pickup Zones

    Both apps operate at Cairo International Airport, but drivers cannot stop directly outside arrivals due to airport traffic-police rules. Riders are routed to a designated pickup point, usually in the multi-story parking area or a marked curb a short walk from the terminal.

    The app shows walking directions to the pickup point once a driver is matched, and it helps to keep the phone connected via airport Wi-Fi or a local SIM so the map updates. Meeting the driver at the wrong spot is a common source of cancelled airport trips.

    Street Taxis Still Run on Negotiated Fares

    Cairo's black-and-white and white taxis, most without working meters in practice, still rely on negotiating the fare before the ride starts. Tourists and even residents unfamiliar with typical local prices often end up paying two to three times a fair rate.

    App-based rides largely solved this by fixing the price in advance, which is the main reason Uber and Careem overtook street taxis for daily use in Cairo, especially among women traveling alone and visitors unfamiliar with the city's geography.

    Cash Remains a Widely Used Payment Method

    Unlike many Western markets where ride-hailing is card-only, both Uber and Careem in Egypt let riders pay the driver in cash at the end of the trip while still booking and tracking through the app. This suits Egypt's largely cash-based economy.

    Card payment and in-app wallets are available and useful for riders without local currency on hand, such as new arrivals. Drivers sometimes prefer cash because card payouts can take longer to settle, so having small denominations ready speeds up the end of a ride.

    In-App Trip Sharing Adds a Real Safety Layer

    Both apps let riders share a live trip link with a friend or family member, showing the car's real-time location, driver name, photo, and license plate. This is a meaningful safety feature in a large city where street taxi details are otherwise unverifiable.

    Egypt's Ministry of Interior has worked with ride-hailing platforms on driver background checks and vehicle registration verification, which is part of why the apps are generally viewed as safer than hailing an unknown taxi off the street, particularly at night.

    Cancellation Fees Apply After a Short Grace Period

    Both apps charge a small cancellation fee if a rider cancels a few minutes after a driver has been assigned and is already en route, since the driver has committed time and fuel to the pickup. Cancelling immediately after booking is usually free.

    Riders can dispute an unfair cancellation fee through in-app support, especially if the driver did not move toward the pickup point or asked the rider to cancel to avoid a low fare on their end, a known workaround some drivers use.

    Tipping Is Optional but Increasingly Common

    Tipping is not built into either app's default checkout in Egypt the way it is in the US, and many local riders do not tip at all for standard trips. It is not expected, and drivers will not ask for one directly.

    That said, rounding up a fare or handing a small cash tip for a driver who helped with heavy luggage, waited patiently, or drove a long distance in bad traffic is appreciated and increasingly normal, particularly among tourists and business travelers.

    Careem Often Runs Cheaper Than Uber for the Same Trip

    Riders comparing both apps for an identical route in Cairo frequently find Careem quoting a lower fare, sometimes by twenty to thirty percent, reflecting differences in each platform's local pricing algorithm and driver commission structure rather than car quality.

    The gap is not fixed and shifts with time of day, demand, and ongoing promotions, so it is worth opening both apps and comparing the quoted price before booking, especially for longer cross-city trips where the percentage difference translates into real money.

    InDrive Lets Riders Negotiate Their Own Fare

    InDrive operates in Cairo as a third major option with a different model: riders propose their own price for a trip, and nearby drivers can accept, counter, or decline it. This hybrid of the old negotiation culture and app-based booking has gained a loyal following.

    It can produce lower fares than Uber or Careem for riders willing to negotiate, but wait times are less predictable since a driver must actively accept the offered price. It is most useful for point-to-point trips where riders know a fair local rate.

    Both Apps Work Reliably Outside Cairo Too

    Uber and Careem operate in Alexandria, and in a more limited way in cities like Mansoura and along parts of the North Coast during summer season. Coverage and driver density are far lower than in greater Cairo, so wait times run longer.

    In tourist-heavy Red Sea cities like Hurghada and Sharm El Sheikh, ride-hailing coverage is patchy and many visitors still rely on hotel transfers or negotiated taxis, so it is worth checking app availability in the specific resort area before counting on it.

    A Local SIM or eSIM Solves the Connectivity Problem

    Both apps need a working data connection to function properly, and relying only on hotel or airport Wi-Fi limits use once out on the street. Egyptian carriers sell tourist SIM cards with data packages at the airport arrivals hall and in city kiosks.

    An eSIM purchased before arrival, where the phone supports it, avoids the arrivals-hall queue entirely and activates on landing. Either option is worth setting up on day one, since it also enables offline map caching that helps confirm pickup points.

    Pin Placement Accuracy Matters More in Cairo

    Cairo's street numbering and building addressing are inconsistent in many neighborhoods, so GPS pin placement often matters more than the typed address. Riders should drag the map pin to the exact building entrance rather than trusting an auto-suggested address.

    Landmark-based pickup, such as naming a well-known cafe, mall entrance, or mosque near the actual location, is common practice and often communicated to the driver directly through the in-app chat or call feature once the trip is matched.

    Rating Systems Run Both Ways

    Riders rate drivers after every trip, and drivers rate riders too, on a five-star scale visible to the other side only in aggregate. A consistently low rider rating can make it harder to get accepted quickly, especially during high-demand periods.

    Common reasons for a low rider score include long waits at pickup, unclear pin locations, or rude behavior, while drivers are rated down for unsafe driving, refusing the app fare in favor of cash negotiation, or taking a noticeably longer route than needed.

    Women-Focused Safety Options Exist on Both Platforms

    Careem introduced features in Egypt allowing female riders to request a female driver where available, and both apps support an in-app emergency button that alerts local authorities or emergency contacts with the trip's live location if something feels wrong.

    Female driver availability is still limited relative to overall driver numbers, so requests may take longer to match or may not be fulfilled during off-peak hours. The emergency and trip-sharing features remain available regardless of driver gender.

    Airport Surcharges Are Built Into the Fare

    Trips starting or ending at Cairo International Airport typically include a small government-mandated surcharge covering the airport's access fees for commercial vehicles, which is reflected automatically in the upfront quoted price rather than added separately.

    This surcharge applies on both apps and is separate from any surge pricing that might also be active, meaning an airport trip during a busy arrival window can combine both factors into a noticeably higher total than the same distance elsewhere in the city.

    Metro Is Often Faster Than a Car in Peak Traffic

    Cairo's metro system, one of the oldest in Africa and the Middle East, runs mostly underground and above the worst surface traffic, making it faster than a ride-hailing car for trips along its lines during rush hour despite lower comfort and no door-to-door service.

    A common practical strategy for cost-conscious travelers is combining the two: taking the metro for the bulk of a cross-city trip, then booking a short Uber or Careem ride for the last stretch to a specific address, cutting both time and fare.

    Driver Refusal to Use the Meter Is a Reportable Issue

    Occasionally an app-matched driver will suggest cancelling and paying an off-app cash fare instead, usually to avoid the platform's commission. This is against both platforms' terms and removes the safety and price protections the app provides.

    Riders who experience this can decline, insist on completing the trip through the app, and report the driver afterward through in-app support, which both companies treat as a policy violation that can affect a driver's standing on the platform.

    Scheduled Rides Help With Early Flights

    Both apps allow booking a ride in advance for a specific pickup time, useful for early morning airport departures when live driver availability is lower and street traffic near the airport can be unpredictable due to security checkpoints.

    A scheduled ride does not guarantee the exact same price as an on-demand booking at that hour, since fares are still calculated closer to pickup time, but it does guarantee a driver is dispatched, which reduces the risk of no cars being available.

    Family and XL Cars Cost More but Save Trips

    A group of four or more with luggage often cannot fit in a standard economy sedan, and both apps flag this at booking by suggesting an XL or family-size vehicle option, priced higher but avoiding the need to book two separate cars.

    For airport transfers with checked bags, choosing the larger tier upfront is usually cheaper and more reliable than hoping a compact car's trunk fits everything, and it avoids the awkward situation of a driver arriving with insufficient space.

    Promo Codes and In-App Credits Cut Costs

    Both apps regularly run promotional discounts for new users and periodic campaigns for existing ones, distributed through the app's promotions tab, email, or partnerships with banks and telecom providers offering ride credit as a loyalty perk.

    Comparing an active promo code between the two apps before booking a longer or more expensive trip, such as an airport transfer, can meaningfully lower the cost, and codes are typically entered in the payment section before confirming the ride.

    Driver Commission Disputes Shape App Perception Locally

    Egyptian drivers on both platforms have periodically protested commission rates and fuel-price-linked fare adjustments, since a large share of each fare goes to the platform rather than the driver. These disputes occasionally affect driver supply on short notice.

    Riders noticing unusually long wait times or driver cancellations in a given period are sometimes seeing the effect of such disputes rather than simple demand spikes, though the apps rarely publicize the underlying cause directly to riders.

    Vehicle Age and Condition Vary Widely

    Because both platforms accept a broad range of registered private vehicles, car age and condition vary more than in stricter markets. An economy-tier car could be a nearly new sedan or an older model that still meets the platform's minimum requirements.

    Riders bothered by this can check the vehicle photo and license plate shown in the app against the car that arrives, both as a safety check and to confirm they are getting into the correct vehicle among several similar-looking cars.

    Egyptian Regulation Formally Recognized Ride-Hailing in 2018

    Egypt's parliament passed a law in 2018 formally regulating ride-hailing platforms, requiring them to register with the government, share data with authorities, and ensure drivers hold proper licensing, ending years of legal ambiguity around the sector's status.

    The law also addressed a long-running dispute with the traditional taxi sector, which had argued app-based drivers operated outside the licensing and tax rules taxi drivers were bound by, by bringing both under clearer, though still not identical, regulatory frameworks.

    Peak-Hour Bridges Are the Main Cairo Bottleneck

    Cairo's Nile crossings, including the 6th of October Bridge and Qasr El Nil Bridge, back up severely during rush hour, and any ride-hailing trip crossing the river during these windows should be budgeted extra time regardless of the app's initial time estimate.

    Both apps' arrival-time estimates are generally accurate for uncongested periods but can understate delays during bridge gridlock, so travelers with fixed appointments or flights should build in a buffer rather than trusting the in-app countdown exactly.

    Language Settings Can Be Switched Instantly

    Both apps default to the phone's system language, and Arabic and English are both fully supported throughout the interface, including driver chat translation prompts in some cases, making it easy for non-Arabic-speaking visitors to navigate the booking flow.

    Switching the in-app language does not affect what the driver sees on their end, and many Cairo drivers speak at least basic English, particularly those who work airport and tourist-heavy routes regularly, easing communication about pickup details.

    Refunds for Overcharges Go Through In-App Support

    If a fare is charged incorrectly, such as a route deviation that was the driver's error rather than traffic, both apps have an in-app dispute process where riders submit the trip details and receive a refund or fare adjustment, typically within a few days.

    Screenshots of the quoted fare versus the charged fare, and a clear description of what went wrong, speed up the review. Egyptian consumer protection law also applies to digital services, giving riders a formal complaint path if in-app support is unresponsive.

    Neither App Fully Replaced the Taxi Meter Debate

    Cairo governorate has periodically pushed initiatives to modernize the traditional taxi fleet with working meters and standardized fares, partly in response to competition from ride-hailing apps that made price transparency the expected norm for a generation of riders.

    Uptake of metered white taxis has been uneven, and many riders in 2026 still default to Uber or Careem specifically because the fare is guaranteed upfront, a trust gap that traditional taxi reform efforts have not fully closed despite official pushes.

    Booking Ahead Beats Street-Hailing for First-Time Visitors

    For anyone visiting Cairo for the first time, booking through Uber or Careem from the airport onward avoids the two biggest first-day risks: overpaying an unfamiliar street taxi and struggling to communicate a destination without shared language or a clear address.

    Setting the destination as text in the app, confirming the fare before accepting, and using the trip-share feature with a contact back home are the three habits that make ride-hailing in Cairo close to as predictable as in any major Western city.

    FAQ

    Is Uber or Careem cheaper in Cairo?

    Careem is often 20-30% cheaper than Uber for the same route, though the gap varies by time of day and current promotions, so it is worth comparing both apps before booking.

    Can I pay for Uber or Careem in cash in Cairo?

    Yes, both apps accept cash payment to the driver at the end of the trip, in addition to card and in-app wallet options.

    Do Uber and Careem work at Cairo International Airport?

    Yes, both operate at the airport but drivers must use a designated pickup zone rather than stopping directly at arrivals, so riders should follow the in-app walking directions.

    Is it safer to use Uber or Careem than a street taxi in Cairo?

    Generally yes, since both apps show the driver's identity, vehicle details, and live trip location, and support trip-sharing with a contact, features street taxis do not offer.

    Do I need to tip Uber or Careem drivers in Egypt?

    Tipping is optional and not built into the standard checkout, but rounding up or giving a small cash tip for good service, heavy luggage, or long trips is appreciated.

    Why does the fare go up during rush hour in Cairo?

    Both apps use surge pricing when demand outstrips available drivers, which happens routinely during Cairo's morning and evening rush hours and bridge congestion.

    Can I book Uber or Careem in Alexandria and other Egyptian cities?

    Yes, both operate in Alexandria with somewhat lower driver density than Cairo, and coverage is more limited in smaller cities and Red Sea resort towns.

    What is InDrive and how is it different from Uber and Careem?

    InDrive lets riders propose their own fare for a trip, which nearby drivers can accept or counter, unlike Uber and Careem's fixed algorithmic pricing.

    Is it legal to use Uber and Careem in Egypt?

    Yes, ride-hailing has been formally regulated under Egyptian law since 2018, requiring platforms to register with the government and drivers to hold proper licensing.

    What should I do if a driver asks to cancel and pay cash off the app?

    Decline and insist on completing the trip through the app, since this removes the platform's price and safety protections; report the driver afterward through in-app support.

    Are there cancellation fees on Uber and Careem in Cairo?

    Yes, a small fee applies if you cancel a few minutes after a driver has been assigned and is already en route; cancelling immediately after booking is usually free.

    Can I request a female driver in Cairo?

    Careem offers a female-driver request option in Egypt where available, though availability is limited relative to overall driver numbers and requests may take longer to match.

    Do I need a local SIM card for Uber and Careem to work well?

    A local SIM or eSIM with a data plan is strongly recommended since both apps need a reliable connection, and relying only on Wi-Fi limits use once outside a building.

    Is the metro faster than Uber or Careem in Cairo traffic?

    For trips along its lines during rush hour, yes, since the metro avoids surface traffic entirely, though it lacks door-to-door convenience.

    What happens if I'm overcharged on a trip?

    You can dispute the fare through in-app support with screenshots of the quoted versus charged price, and both apps typically process a refund or adjustment within a few days.
