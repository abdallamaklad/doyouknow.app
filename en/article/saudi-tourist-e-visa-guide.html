    • The E-Visa Replaced a Slower Paper Process in 2019

    Saudi Arabia launched its electronic tourist visa in September 2019, opening the country to leisure travelers for the first time outside religious pilgrimage. Before that, entry required an invitation, a business sponsor, or an Umrah booking. The e-visa removed that requirement for a defined list of nationalities.

    The system runs through a single government portal rather than embassies or consulates. Applicants fill in passport and travel details online, pay the fee, and receive a decision without visiting an office. It was one of the first steps in the Vision 2030 tourism push.

    Eligibility Runs Through an Official Country List

    Only citizens of countries named on the official eligible list can apply for the e-visa; everyone else needs a different visa route. The list has expanded repeatedly since 2019, most recently adding nations across Europe, Asia and elsewhere, and now covers well over sixty countries.

    Because the list changes, travelers should check the current version on the official visa.visitsaudi.com portal before booking flights rather than relying on older articles. Citizens of GCC states and holders of valid US, UK or Schengen visas also have separate entry pathways.

    The Visa Grants One Year of Multiple Entries

    Once issued, the tourist e-visa is valid for one full year from the date of approval, not from the date of first entry. Holders can enter and leave the country repeatedly during that year rather than being limited to a single trip.

    This multiple-entry structure suits people combining a Saudi stop with travel elsewhere in the region, or those who want to visit more than once for work-adjacent tourism, family visits or Umrah, without reapplying and paying the fee again each time.

    Each Individual Stay Is Capped at Ninety Days

    Although the visa lasts a year, no single visit can exceed ninety days. Travelers must leave the country before that limit and can then re-enter on the same visa as long as it has not expired and cumulative rules are respected.

    The ninety-day figure counts consecutive days inside the Kingdom on one entry, not total days used across the year. There is no fixed annual cap on total days published, but immigration officers can question patterns that look like disguised residency rather than tourism.

    Application Happens Entirely Through One Official Portal

    The only official channel is visa.visitsaudi.com, run by the Saudi Tourism Authority. Applicants create an account, enter passport details, upload a photo, answer health and travel history questions, and pay online. No embassy appointment or physical document submission is needed.

    Third-party websites that charge extra fees for the same service exist and are not affiliated with the government. Applying directly through the official portal avoids markup charges and reduces the risk of data being handled by an unverified intermediary.

    A Valid Passport Must Cover Six Months

    Applicants need a passport valid for at least six months beyond the planned date of entry into Saudi Arabia. A passport nearing expiry is one of the most common reasons an otherwise straightforward application gets rejected at the border or during review.

    The passport must also have at least one blank page for entry stamps, even though most processing is electronic. Travelers renewing a passport shortly before a trip should apply for the e-visa only after receiving the new document, not before.

    Applicants Upload a Digital Passport-Style Photo

    The online form requires a recent, clear photo meeting standard passport-photo rules: plain background, front-facing, no headwear except for religious purposes, and no sunglasses. Poor-quality or outdated photos can delay approval or trigger a request for resubmission.

    There is no requirement to visit a photo studio physically listed by the government; any photo meeting the technical specifications on the portal is accepted. Applicants should check file size and format requirements listed on the site before uploading.

    The Visa Fee Is Charged in Saudi Riyals

    The e-visa fee is charged in Saudi riyals at checkout on the official portal, and it bundles the visa charge together with a mandatory travel health insurance premium. Card payment is standard; the exact total can shift slightly with exchange rates for foreign cards.

    Because the fee includes insurance, applicants should not expect a separate insurance purchase step. Refunds are generally not offered if a trip is cancelled after approval, so travelers should confirm travel plans are firm before submitting payment.

    Travel Health Insurance Is Bundled Into the Fee

    Every e-visa purchase automatically includes basic travel health insurance covering the holder for the duration of the stay. This was designed to guarantee visitors have some medical coverage during their trip without requiring them to arrange a separate policy.

    The bundled policy is typically a baseline product and may not cover every scenario, such as pre-existing conditions or high-cost evacuation. Travelers wanting broader coverage can buy supplemental travel insurance separately before departure.

    Processing Usually Finishes Within Minutes to Days

    Many e-visa applications are approved within minutes because the system checks eligibility and documents automatically. Some cases require manual review and take longer, typically a few business days, especially when submitted information needs verification against passport records.

    Applying at least several days before departure, rather than the same day, gives enough buffer for the rare cases that need extra review. The portal sends status updates to the email address used during registration.

    Approval Arrives by Email as a PDF

    Once approved, the e-visa is sent as a PDF document to the applicant's registered email, not stamped physically into the passport in advance. Travelers should print a copy or save it on a phone to present at check-in and immigration.

    Airlines typically ask to see the e-visa approval before allowing boarding, since they are liable for passengers who arrive without valid entry documents. Keeping both a digital and printed copy avoids problems if a phone battery dies or a file will not open.

    The E-Visa Also Permits Umrah Outside Hajj Season

    Unlike the earlier system that required a separate Umrah visa through a licensed agent, the tourist e-visa now allows holders to perform Umrah at any time outside the Hajj season. This merged tourism and religious visitation into a single visa product.

    This change was significant because it let independent travelers perform Umrah without booking through an approved travel group, provided they still follow the rules and timing set by the religious affairs authorities for the pilgrimage rites themselves.

    Tourists May Combine Sightseeing With Umrah Rites

    A single trip can legally include both standard tourism, such as visiting AlUla or the Red Sea coast, and Umrah in Mecca and Medina, since one e-visa now covers both purposes. Travelers no longer need to choose one category over the other.

    This flexibility has made Saudi Arabia more attractive for combined faith and leisure itineraries. Visitors should still plan around religious sensitivities in Mecca and Medina, where dress and behavior expectations are stricter than in purely secular tourist areas.

    Entry Is Possible Through Named Airports and Land Points

    E-visa holders can enter through major international airports including Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam, as well as designated land and sea border points shared with neighboring GCC countries. Not every minor crossing accepts e-visa entry, so checking the approved list matters.

    Travelers arriving overland from Bahrain via the King Fahd Causeway, for example, should confirm the specific crossing is authorized for e-visa holders before planning that route, since border infrastructure and rules can differ from air arrivals.

    Non-Muslims Cannot Enter Mecca or Central Medina

    Regardless of visa type, non-Muslims are barred from entering the city of Mecca and the restricted central zone of Medina around the Prophet's Mosque. Road signs and checkpoints direct non-Muslim travelers around these areas onto bypass routes.

    This restriction predates the e-visa and applies to every visitor category, including tourists and residents. Travelers planning a route that passes near Mecca should build in the detour time, since checkpoints actively enforce the boundary.

    Riyadh, Jeddah and Other Cities Remain Fully Open

    Outside the restricted zones around Mecca and central Medina, Saudi Arabia's cities are open to all e-visa holders regardless of religion. Riyadh, Jeddah, AlUla, Abha and the Red Sea coast are standard stops on tourist itineraries with no religious entry restriction.

    Most museums, historical sites and entertainment venues built under the Vision 2030 tourism push, such as Boulevard Riyadh City and the AlUla heritage sites, welcome all visitors and are a major part of the marketing behind the e-visa program.

    Modest Dress Is Required in Public Spaces

    Saudi Arabia expects modest dress from all visitors in public: shoulders and knees generally covered for both men and women, and swimwear reserved for pools, private beaches or resort areas rather than public streets or malls.

    Enforcement has relaxed considerably compared with a decade ago, and there is no legal minimum dress code written specifically for foreign tourists, but social norms still shape what is comfortable to wear, particularly outside cosmopolitan hotel and resort zones.

    Abayas Are No Longer Mandatory for Foreign Women

    Since 2019, foreign women visiting Saudi Arabia are no longer legally required to wear an abaya, the loose black cloak once mandatory in public. Many women still choose loose, modest clothing out of comfort and cultural respect rather than legal obligation.

    This change was one of the most visible signals of the social reforms accompanying the tourism opening. Saudi women's dress rules are separate and set by different social and religious norms than those applied to foreign visitors.

    Alcohol Remains Illegal Throughout the Country

    Saudi Arabia enforces a nationwide ban on alcohol production, sale, importation and consumption, with no exception for tourists, hotels or diplomatic zones open to the public. Carrying alcohol across the border or brewing it privately can lead to serious legal penalties.

    This is one rule that has not changed alongside the tourism reforms. Visitors coming from countries where alcohol is routine socially should plan accordingly, since even small quantities found at customs can result in confiscation, fines or detention.

    Unmarried Couples Can Share Hotel Rooms Since 2019

    As part of the same reform package that introduced the tourist e-visa, hotels stopped requiring proof of marriage for unmarried couples to book a shared room. Previously, mixed-gender couples without documentation could be turned away at check-in.

    This change removed a significant practical barrier for foreign couples and tour groups. Individual hotels may still ask for identification matching the booking, but marital status documentation is no longer a legal requirement for check-in.

    Women No Longer Need a Male Guardian to Travel

    Saudi Arabia lifted the requirement for adult women, including Saudi citizens, to obtain male guardian permission to travel internationally in 2019. Foreign women visiting on a tourist e-visa were never subject to Saudi guardianship rules in the first place.

    This reform primarily affected Saudi women's own mobility, but it is frequently misunderstood by travelers as also applying restrictions to foreign visitors. Foreign women can enter, move around and exit the country independently on their own e-visa.

    A Tourist Visa Does Not Authorize Paid Work

    The e-visa is strictly for tourism, Umrah, family visits and short business meetings that do not involve local employment. Taking paid work, freelancing for a Saudi client on the ground, or running a business requires a separate work visa and sponsor.

    Immigration authorities distinguish between attending a conference or meeting, which is allowed, and performing billable labor inside the country, which is not. Violating this distinction can result in deportation and future entry bans.

    Overstaying Triggers Fines and Possible Entry Bans

    Staying past the ninety-day limit for a single visit results in daily fines that accumulate the longer the overstay continues. Prolonged or repeated overstays can lead to deportation and a ban on future entry to Saudi Arabia for a set period.

    Genuine emergencies, such as a medical situation preventing travel, can sometimes be addressed through Jawazat, the immigration authority, before the deadline passes. Waiting until after the visa has expired to seek help makes the situation harder to resolve.

    The E-Visa Differs From a Dedicated Umrah Visa

    Travelers who prefer to book through a licensed Umrah operator, or who come from a country not on the e-visa eligible list, can still apply for a traditional Umrah visa, which follows different rules, seasons and agent requirements.

    The Umrah-specific visa typically ties the traveler to a registered agency itinerary and accommodation, while the e-visa gives independent travelers more freedom over their schedule. Both routes lead to valid, legal Umrah performance when used correctly.

    GCC Citizens Use a Separate Unified Entry Route

    Citizens of the Gulf Cooperation Council states, including the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Oman, enter Saudi Arabia under GCC citizen entry arrangements rather than the tourist e-visa system, generally with fewer document requirements at the border.

    This distinction matters for dual nationals or expatriate residents of GCC countries, since holding residency in a GCC state does not automatically confer the same entry rights as holding that state's citizenship.

    Rejections Usually Trace Back to Document Mismatches

    Common reasons an e-visa application is refused include a passport nearing expiry, a photo that fails to meet specifications, mismatched personal details between the passport and the form, or a nationality that has been temporarily suspended from the eligible list.

    Applicants who are refused can generally correct the error and reapply rather than facing a permanent ban, provided the underlying issue was administrative rather than a security or criminal-record concern flagged during review.

    Local SIM Cards Are Available on Arrival

    Tourist SIM cards and eSIMs from Saudi carriers such as STC, Mobily and Zain are sold at airport kiosks and require only the passport used for entry. Data packages are geared toward short visits and are usually straightforward to activate.

    Having a local number and data plan makes it easier to use ride-hailing apps, maps and payment apps that expect a Saudi phone number for verification, and it avoids the higher cost of international roaming for the length of a trip.

    Photography of People Requires Their Consent

    Photographing government buildings, military sites and strangers, especially women, without permission is discouraged and in some cases legally restricted. Tourist attractions and landscapes are generally fine to photograph freely, but common courtesy applies to photographing people directly.

    Some cultural and religious sites post explicit no-photography signs, particularly around prayer areas. Following posted signage and asking before photographing individuals avoids both social friction and possible intervention from site security staff.

    Government Sites Warn About Unofficial Visa Agents

    The Saudi Tourism Authority and immigration channels repeatedly warn travelers to apply only through the official visa.visitsaudi.com portal. Numerous unofficial third-party sites mimic the design of government portals while charging significant markups for the same free-to-process application.

    Beyond the extra cost, submitting sensitive passport data to an unverified third party carries privacy and fraud risk. Checking that the URL matches the official domain before entering any personal or payment information is a basic but important precaution.

    Hajj Season Closes the Country to Regular Tourism

    During the Hajj season, the tourist e-visa cannot be used to perform Hajj itself, and entry for general tourism is tightly managed to prioritize pilgrims. Hajj requires its own separate, quota-based visa process managed through official pilgrim group channels.

    Travelers planning a trip should check the Islamic calendar dates for Hajj each year, since the period shifts annually, and expect that regular tourist travel to Mecca and Medina areas will be restricted or unavailable during that window.

    Currency Declaration Applies Above a Set Threshold

    Travelers carrying large amounts of cash, generally above the equivalent of 60,000 Saudi riyals, are required to declare it to customs on entry or exit. This applies to all currencies combined, not just riyals, and covers cash, checks and similar instruments.

    Failing to declare amounts above the threshold can result in confiscation of the funds and legal penalties, regardless of whether the money was earned legitimately. Declaring simply requires filling in a customs form, which takes only a few minutes.

    Extensions and Status Changes Require Immigration Approval

    Extending a stay beyond what the e-visa allows, or converting a tourist visit into another status such as work or residency, is not automatic and must go through Jawazat, the General Directorate of Passports, rather than being handled at the airport.

    Because approval is discretionary and processing takes time, travelers who anticipate needing more time in the country should raise the request well before the visa or the ninety-day stay limit runs out, rather than waiting until the last days.

    FAQ

    Who is eligible for the Saudi tourist e-visa?

    Citizens of countries on the official eligible list published at visa.visitsaudi.com can apply. The list has grown since 2019 and now covers more than sixty nations; travelers should verify their country's current status before booking.

    How long is the e-visa valid?

    The e-visa is valid for one year from approval and allows multiple entries, but each individual stay is limited to ninety days before the traveler must leave and can re-enter later.

    Can the tourist e-visa be used for Umrah?

    Yes, since the reforms of 2019 the tourist e-visa also permits Umrah at any time outside the Hajj season, without needing a separate Umrah-specific visa or agent booking.

    Where do I apply for the e-visa?

    Apply only through the official government portal at visa.visitsaudi.com. Third-party sites that mimic this portal charge extra fees and are not part of the official process.

    Does the e-visa fee include insurance?

    Yes, the fee charged at checkout bundles a basic travel health insurance policy covering the holder for the length of the trip, alongside the visa charge itself.

    Can foreign women travel to Saudi Arabia without a male guardian?

    Yes. The male guardian travel restriction applied to Saudi women and was lifted in 2019. Foreign women visiting on a tourist e-visa have never needed guardian permission to enter or travel independently.

    Is alcohol available anywhere for tourists?

    No. Alcohol is illegal throughout Saudi Arabia for everyone, including tourists, with no exception for hotels or resort areas open to the public.

    What happens if I overstay my ninety-day limit?

    Overstaying triggers accumulating daily fines, and prolonged or repeated violations can lead to deportation and a temporary ban on re-entering Saudi Arabia.

    Can I enter Mecca on a tourist e-visa?

    Only if you are Muslim. Non-Muslims are barred from entering Mecca and the restricted central zone of Medina regardless of visa type; this rule applies to every visitor category.

    Do unmarried couples need to show proof of marriage at hotels?

    No. Since the 2019 reforms, hotels no longer require unmarried couples to prove marital status to share a room, though identification matching the booking may still be requested.

    How much cash can I bring in without declaring it?

    Amounts above roughly 60,000 Saudi riyals in combined currencies, cash or equivalent instruments must be declared to customs on entry or exit; undeclared excess can be confiscated.

    Can I work in Saudi Arabia on a tourist e-visa?

    No. The tourist e-visa does not authorize paid employment or freelance work performed inside the country. A separate work visa and sponsor are required for that.

    What passport validity do I need?

    Your passport must remain valid for at least six months beyond your planned entry date, with at least one blank page available for entry processing.

    Are GCC citizens required to get the tourist e-visa?

    No. Citizens of Gulf Cooperation Council states use separate GCC citizen entry arrangements rather than the tourist e-visa system, generally with simpler border requirements.

    What should I do if my e-visa application is rejected?

    Check for common issues like passport expiry, a non-compliant photo, or mismatched details, correct them, and reapply through the official portal; most rejections are administrative and fixable.
