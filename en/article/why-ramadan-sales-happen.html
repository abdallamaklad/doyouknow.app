Why Ramadan Sales Happen, and What Actually Gets Discounted

    • Ramadan is the region's single biggest retail season

    For UAE, Saudi, and Egyptian retailers, Ramadan functions the way the end-of-year holiday period does in Western markets, a concentrated stretch when household spending rises sharply and stores plan their entire annual promotional calendar around it.

    The month combines two distinct shopping behaviours, a spike in daily grocery and food spending for iftar and suhoor, and a separate wave of gift, fashion, and gadget buying building toward Eid, which is why Ramadan sales don't look like one continuous discount but several overlapping campaigns.

    Why the sales calendar splits into distinct phases

    Retail planning across the region typically breaks Ramadan into three windows: the run-up week before the month starts, when grocery and essentials promotions peak; the middle weeks, generally the quietest for discretionary spending; and the final ten days, when fashion, gifting, and electronics offers concentrate hardest.

    Understanding which phase a shopper is in matters practically, because the same category discounts very differently depending on the week, and buying at the wrong point in the calendar can mean missing the deepest cut entirely.

    Groceries and dates discount the earliest and the deepest

    Supermarkets across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Egypt run their most aggressive discounting in the week or two before Ramadan begins, on staples like dates, rice, lentils, dairy, and cooking essentials, since these are the highest-volume, highest-certainty purchases of the entire month.

    Dates in particular see the most visible promotional activity of any single product category, with supermarkets stocking dozens of varieties and running loss-leader pricing on at least a few, because dates are the one item almost every household buys regardless of income level.

    Why supermarkets are willing to lose money on dates

    Loss-leader pricing on dates draws shoppers into the store for their broader Ramadan grocery trip, where the retailer recovers margin on the dozens of other items in the same basket, a well-established supermarket strategy applied specifically to the season's signature product.

    This is also why comparing date prices across two or three supermarkets before the season's peak week is one of the more reliably worthwhile price-checking habits, since the spread between the loss-leader price and the regular price can be substantial.

    Abayas and modest fashion follow a different rhythm

    Abaya and modest fashion retailers time their promotional peak to the final two weeks of Ramadan rather than the start, because new collections for Eid prayers and family gatherings are the primary purchase driver, and shoppers deliberately wait to see the newest Eid-specific designs before committing.

    This creates a pattern where early-Ramadan fashion discounts are often on older or end-of-season stock, while the genuinely new Eid collections rarely discount meaningfully until after Eid itself, once the season's main selling window has closed.

    Why new Eid collections rarely discount before the holiday

    Retailers protect full price on freshly launched Eid abayas and outfits because demand for new designs stays high right up to Eid, unlike a category where demand tapers gradually, giving the retailer little commercial reason to discount before the selling window closes.

    The real markdown on that specific new stock typically appears only after Eid, in the week or two following, when the retailer needs to clear remaining inventory before the next seasonal changeover.

    Electronics discount hardest just ahead of Eid, not mid-Ramadan

    Electronics retailers concentrate their steepest promotions in the final week to ten days of Ramadan, timed deliberately to the pre-Eid gifting and upgrade cycle, since phones, televisions, and home appliances are common Eid gift and home-refresh purchases.

    Buying electronics in the early or middle weeks of Ramadan typically means paying closer to standard price, since the category's real seasonal discount hasn't opened up yet at that point in the calendar.

    Why electronics wait for the pre-Eid window specifically

    Electronics retailers coordinate closely with brand manufacturers on regional promotional calendars, and the biggest joint campaigns are deliberately scheduled to land in the final Ramadan stretch, maximising the overlap between Ramadan's shopping mood and Eid's gift-buying urgency in a single push.

    This coordination also explains why the same model can appear at a similar discount across multiple competing electronics retailers simultaneously in that final window, since the promotional calendar is often set at the brand level, not purely by an individual store.

    Which categories barely discount at all during Ramadan

    Fresh produce and meat, largely because prices already fluctuate with regional demand rather than promotional planning, and category staples that see spiking demand, like specific brands of dairy or certain cuts of meat, often hold steady or even rise slightly rather than discount.

    Furniture, home renovation, and big-ticket categories not tied to gifting or the Eid calendar also tend to see little to no meaningful Ramadan-specific promotion, since demand for them isn't seasonally driven the way food and fashion are.

    Why demand-driven staples sometimes get more expensive, not cheaper

    Certain high-demand Ramadan staples, particularly specific dairy products, spices, and cooking oils, see demand spikes that outpace supply in the short window before and during the month, which occasionally pushes prices up rather than into promotion.

    Regional consumer protection authorities in the UAE and Saudi Arabia monitor this specific pattern, since unjustified price hikes on essential goods during Ramadan are a recurring complaint category they actively track each year.

    The retail logic behind Ramadan's promotional intensity

    Retailers treat Ramadan as a volume event rather than a margin event for many categories, accepting thinner profit per item because the total number of transactions across the month is high enough to make the aggregate quarter's revenue depend heavily on this single season.

    This is structurally similar to how a Western retailer treats the year-end holiday period, a season where discounting deeply on traffic-driving categories is worth it because it pulls in overall spend across the whole store, not just the discounted item.

    How the fasting-hours shift changes shopping behaviour itself

    Shopping activity in physical stores shifts heavily to the hours after iftar, with malls and supermarkets across the UAE and Saudi Arabia routinely extending opening hours late into the night to capture the post-sunset surge in foot traffic.

    Retailers plan flash promotions and in-store activations specifically around this post-iftar window, since it's when the largest concentration of shoppers with both time and appetite for browsing is physically in the store.

    Online shopping patterns shift too, not just in-store

    E-commerce traffic across the region shows a pronounced late-night spike during Ramadan, roughly between iftar and suhoor, a window when many online retailers schedule flash deals and limited-time app notifications specifically to match when shoppers are actually browsing.

    This shift means the best time to catch a genuine flash discount online during Ramadan is often well after conventional evening hours, closer to midnight or the early hours before suhoor, rather than during the daytime fasting hours.

    Historical Ramadan spending patterns across the region

    Regional retail associations and central bank consumption data have consistently shown household spending rising noticeably during Ramadan compared with the months immediately before and after it, concentrated heavily in food, apparel, and gifting categories.

    This pattern has held steady across recent years even as e-commerce has taken a growing share of that overall spend, with online retail specifically capturing more of the Ramadan surge each year relative to physical stores.

    Why the Islamic lunar calendar makes planning harder for retailers

    Because Ramadan shifts roughly ten to eleven days earlier each Gregorian year, retailers can't simply repeat the previous year's exact promotional calendar, they have to re-plan around a Ramadan that might fall in a different season with different weather, school schedules, and daylight hours.

    A Ramadan falling during shorter fasting days versus longer summer fasting days changes shopping-hour patterns meaningfully, which retail planners factor into how aggressively and when they schedule their sales.

    Grocery delivery apps and their own Ramadan pricing tactics

    Grocery delivery platforms across the UAE and Saudi Arabia run their own layer of Ramadan promotions on top of in-store supermarket discounts, including free delivery thresholds and app-exclusive bundle pricing on iftar staples that may differ from the physical store's shelf price.

    Comparing the delivery app's bundled price against the same items bought individually in-store is worth doing specifically during Ramadan, since bundle pricing can sometimes work out more expensive than assembling the same basket manually.

    Restaurant and iftar buffet pricing follows its own logic

    Hotel and restaurant iftar and suhoor offers in the UAE and Saudi Arabia are priced as a dedicated seasonal product line, not a discount on regular menu pricing, meaning comparing an iftar buffet's value against the venue's normal à la carte prices isn't really an apples-to-apples comparison.

    Early booking for well-known iftar venues often carries a lower fixed price than booking closer to the date, since demand for popular locations concentrates heavily in the first and last weeks of the month.

    How Ramadan sales differ between the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Egypt

    The UAE and Saudi markets, with higher disposable income concentration, see the heaviest promotional spend on electronics, fashion, and travel, while Egypt's Ramadan retail activity concentrates more heavily on food staples and household essentials, reflecting a different weight of consumer spending priorities.

    Egypt also sees a distinct pattern of government and manufacturer price-monitoring initiatives specifically for essential Ramadan goods, aimed at controlling inflation on staples during the month rather than promoting discretionary discounting.

    Eid gift-giving as the final purchase driver

    The days immediately before Eid see a distinct spike in gift purchases, particularly for children, driven by the regional tradition of Eidiya and new clothes for the holiday, a purchase pattern separate from and additional to the general Ramadan grocery and fashion cycle.

    Retailers targeting this window often bundle gift-wrapping, gift cards, or children's product promotions specifically in the final Ramadan days, distinct from the broader-category sales running earlier in the month.

    Travel and airfare pricing around Ramadan and Eid

    Airfare and travel packages for the Eid break, when many residents travel home or take a family holiday, tend to rise rather than discount in the final week of Ramadan, following standard peak-demand pricing rather than a seasonal sale pattern.

    Booking Eid travel well before Ramadan begins, rather than waiting for a Ramadan sale that doesn't really apply to this category, is generally the more cost-effective approach based on how airline pricing responds to demand surges.

    Why comparing prices to pre-Ramadan levels matters more than the discount label

    A percentage-off sticker is only meaningful relative to the price the item was actually selling at before the promotion began, and some retailers quietly raise a price shortly before Ramadan so the subsequent markdown looks larger than the real saving.

    Checking a price-tracking tool or simply recalling what an item cost a month or two earlier is a more reliable way to judge whether a Ramadan discount is genuine than trusting the percentage figure printed on the tag.

    The role of loyalty programme points during Ramadan

    Supermarket and retail loyalty programmes across the region commonly run point multiplier promotions during Ramadan specifically, since the season's higher basket sizes make it a strategically valuable time to deepen customer engagement with a rewards programme.

    Timing a larger, planned Ramadan grocery run to coincide with a loyalty programme's multiplier period, when advertised in advance, can meaningfully add to the value of a purchase beyond the sticker discount itself.

    Why some retailers front-load discounts and others hold back

    Cash-flow-sensitive smaller retailers sometimes discount earlier in Ramadan to generate immediate revenue, while larger retailers with stronger balance sheets can afford to hold their steepest cuts for the final pre-Eid window when demand and urgency are highest.

    This creates genuine variation in when the best deal on a specific item appears depending on the size and financial position of the retailer selling it, not just the product category itself.

    The difference between a genuine clearance and a manufactured Ramadan sale

    A genuine clearance discount is typically tied to a specific, limited stock quantity and doesn't reappear once sold out, while a manufactured Ramadan sale is often just the retailer's standard price with seasonal marketing language attached, restocked continuously throughout the month.

    Checking whether the same discounted item reappears in stock repeatedly across the month, rather than selling out and staying gone, is a practical signal for telling these two apart.

    Card issuer and BNPL-linked Ramadan promotions

    Banks and BNPL providers in the UAE and Saudi Arabia commonly run Ramadan-specific cashback or discount campaigns tied to using a particular card or instalment app at partner retailers, layering an additional saving on top of the retailer's own promotion.

    These campaigns are typically announced ahead of the month and tied to specific merchant categories, so checking a bank's or BNPL app's own Ramadan campaign page before a planned purchase can add real value beyond the storefront discount.

    Why comparing category discounts, not just headline percentages, matters

    A store advertising up to seventy per cent off tells a shopper very little, since that ceiling might apply to a narrow clearance selection while the bulk of the catalogue sits at a much smaller discount, a pattern common across Ramadan marketing broadly.

    Checking the specific discount on the exact item wanted, rather than trusting the largest number in the campaign's headline, is the more reliable way to judge whether a particular Ramadan sale is actually worth acting on.

    What tends to genuinely surprise first-time regional shoppers

    New residents or first-time regional shoppers often expect Ramadan to be one uniform discount period across every category, and are surprised to find fashion holding firm mid-month while groceries discount early and electronics wait until the very end.

    Understanding this staggered structure in advance changes purchase timing meaningfully, letting a shopper plan the grocery run early, wait on the fashion purchase, and time the electronics upgrade to the final pre-Eid week specifically.

    How to plan a Ramadan budget around this staggered timing

    A practical approach splits the Ramadan budget into its natural categories ahead of the month: groceries and dates bought in the pre-Ramadan discount window, fashion held until the final two weeks when new Eid stock and any resulting clearance both become visible, and electronics reserved specifically for the pre-Eid final stretch.

    This staggered spending plan also smooths cash flow across the month rather than concentrating the entire Ramadan and Eid budget into a single late rush, which is when prices on non-discounted, high-demand items tend to be least favourable.

    Why post-Eid clearance is often the real bargain window

    The genuinely deepest discounts on fashion and gift categories frequently land in the one to two weeks after Eid, when retailers need to clear remaining seasonal stock before shifting to their next collection, a window many shoppers overlook because their attention has already moved past the holiday.

    For a shopper without a specific need to have an item before Eid itself, waiting for this post-Eid window can outperform even the final pre-Eid sale in terms of actual discount depth.

    How perfume and gifting-adjacent categories move during the month

    Perfume, often bought as an Eid gift or for personal use during the holiday, sees promotional activity cluster in the final pre-Eid window similarly to fashion and electronics, with duty-free and mall retailers running bundle offers rather than straightforward percentage discounts.

    Because perfume pricing already varies significantly by concentration and channel outside of any Ramadan promotion, a genuinely good Ramadan perfume deal is easier to judge by comparing the bundle's per-item value than by the headline discount alone.

    Why comparing across the same retailer over multiple years builds a better sense of what's real

    A shopper who tracks the same retailer's Ramadan pricing across two or three consecutive years develops a much sharper sense of which discounts repeat annually as marketing and which represent a genuinely unusual price drop worth acting on.

    This kind of year-over-year comparison is more reliable than judging a single season's sale in isolation, since it filters out the retailer's routine seasonal marketing language from an actual, non-recurring price event.

    What actually matters when shopping the Ramadan sales calendar

    The single most useful mental model is that Ramadan isn't one sale, it's three: an early grocery and staples window, a quiet middle stretch where little of value discounts, and a final pre-Eid surge covering fashion and electronics, followed by a genuine post-Eid clearance.

    Matching each category's purchase to its actual discount window, rather than assuming any Ramadan-branded promotion is automatically the best price of the season, is what separates a shopper who saves meaningfully from one who simply buys on impulse when a banner says sale.

    FAQ

    When do Ramadan sales in the UAE and Saudi Arabia actually start?

    Grocery and essentials discounting typically starts one to two weeks before Ramadan begins, while fashion and electronics see their steepest cuts concentrate in the final ten days of the month, closer to Eid.

    Are dates always the cheapest right before Ramadan?

    Generally yes, supermarkets commonly run loss-leader pricing on dates in the run-up week specifically to draw in broader grocery shopping, making that window the most reliable time to buy them.

    Do abaya prices actually drop during Ramadan?

    New Eid-specific collections rarely discount meaningfully until after Eid, while older or end-of-season abaya stock may see earlier markdowns during Ramadan itself.

    Is it better to buy electronics early or late in Ramadan?

    Late. Electronics retailers concentrate their deepest discounts in the final week to ten days before Eid, timed to the gift and upgrade cycle, so early-Ramadan prices are usually closer to standard.

    Why do some grocery prices actually go up during Ramadan instead of down?

    High-demand staples like certain dairy products and spices can see demand outpace supply in the short pre-Ramadan window, occasionally pushing prices up, which regional consumer protection authorities actively monitor.

    Is the biggest discount of the season during Ramadan or right after Eid?

    Often after Eid. Retailers need to clear remaining fashion and gift stock in the week or two following the holiday, which frequently produces deeper markdowns than the pre-Eid sale itself.

    Do online Ramadan sales happen at different times than in-store sales?

    The category timing is similar, but online flash deals specifically cluster in the late-night hours between iftar and suhoor, when e-commerce traffic peaks during Ramadan.

    Do bank and BNPL apps run their own separate Ramadan discounts?

    Yes, many banks and BNPL providers run Ramadan-specific cashback or discount campaigns tied to specific partner merchants, which can stack on top of the retailer's own promotion.

    Why does Ramadan fall on different dates each year?

    Ramadan follows the Islamic lunar calendar, which is shorter than the Gregorian calendar, so the month shifts roughly ten to eleven days earlier each successive Gregorian year.

    Are travel and airfare prices discounted during Ramadan?

    Generally no. Airfare for the Eid travel period tends to rise with peak demand rather than discount, so booking well ahead of Ramadan is usually the more cost-effective approach.

    Do restaurants actually offer good value with their iftar buffets?

    Value varies widely by venue and is priced as a dedicated seasonal product rather than a discount off regular menu pricing, so it's worth comparing across a few venues rather than assuming any iftar offer is inherently a deal.

    How can a shopper tell if a Ramadan discount is genuine?

    Compare the sale price against what the item cost a month or two before Ramadan began, since some retailers raise prices briefly beforehand so the later markdown looks larger than the real saving.

    Does Ramadan retail spending look the same in Egypt as in the UAE and Saudi Arabia?

    Not exactly. Egypt's Ramadan retail activity concentrates more on food staples and household essentials, while UAE and Saudi spending skews more heavily toward electronics, fashion, and travel.

    Why do malls and supermarkets extend their hours during Ramadan?

    Because shopping activity shifts heavily to the hours after iftar, so retailers extend opening times late into the night to capture the post-sunset surge in foot traffic.

    Is perfume a good category to buy during Ramadan sales?

    It can be, particularly as bundle offers cluster in the final pre-Eid window, but since perfume pricing already varies a lot by channel and concentration, comparing the bundle's per-item value matters more than the headline discount.
