    • A Tawkil Is a Notarized Delegation of Authority

    A tawkil is Egypt's legal instrument for one person, the principal, to authorize another, the agent, to act on their behalf in specific legal, financial, or administrative matters. It must be documented in writing and formally notarized to have legal effect before Egyptian authorities.

    Unlike a casual written authorization letter, a tawkil carries binding legal weight once notarized, meaning the agent can sign contracts, complete government paperwork, or represent the principal in ways an informal letter cannot achieve in Egyptian institutions.

    General Tawkil Grants Broad, Wide-Ranging Powers

    A general power of attorney, tawkil aam, gives the agent sweeping authority to act across nearly any legal or financial matter on the principal's behalf, similar in spirit to a durable general power of attorney in Western legal systems.

    Because of its breadth, a general tawkil carries significant risk if given to the wrong person, since the agent could, in theory, sign contracts, manage bank matters, or handle property well beyond what the principal originally intended, making careful agent selection essential.

    Special Tawkil Limits the Agent to Named Actions

    A special or limited power of attorney, tawkil khas, restricts the agent's authority to specific, clearly defined actions, such as selling one named property, collecting a particular payment, or representing the principal in one court case, and nothing beyond that scope.

    Egyptian lawyers and notaries generally recommend a special tawkil over a general one whenever the task at hand is well defined, since it minimizes the agent's ability to act outside the principal's actual intent and reduces exposure if the agent turns out to be untrustworthy.

    Property Tawkil Wording Must Match the Exact Transaction

    For real estate matters specifically, Egyptian notarial practice requires the tawkil's wording to precisely match the actions the agent needs to perform, such as selling, buying, registering, receiving the sale price, signing before notary offices, or dealing with a property developer.

    Generic wording that does not explicitly cover an action, such as receiving payment or dealing with a specific developer, may not be accepted by that party even if the tawkil otherwise appears broad, which is why property lawyers draft these documents carefully rather than using generic templates.

    Notarization Happens at the Real Estate Publicity Department

    A tawkil intended for use in Egypt is formally notarized at the Real Estate Publicity Department, commonly known as Al Shahr Al Aqari, which operates under the Ministry of Justice and runs notary offices across the country handling both property and general legal documents.

    The principal, or in some cases their legal representative, must appear in person at a notary office with valid identification, and the document is registered in the department's official records, giving it the authenticated legal status Egyptian institutions require to act on it.

    An Accredited Translator Is Required for Foreign-Language Principals

    When the principal does not speak Arabic fluently, Egyptian notary offices require a court-accredited translator to be present during the signing, ensuring the principal genuinely understands the document's contents before granting the power of attorney.

    A tawkil cannot be certified without this translator present in such cases, which is a safeguard against foreign buyers, especially in real estate transactions, unknowingly signing away broader authority than they intended due to a language barrier.

    Egyptians Abroad Can Issue a Tawkil at an Egyptian Consulate

    An Egyptian citizen or a foreigner residing outside Egypt who needs to grant power of attorney for use inside the country can sign the document at an Egyptian embassy or consulate abroad, where consular staff notarize it under Egyptian law.

    This consular tawkil typically then needs to be legalized and, once received in Egypt, registered or activated at the relevant local authority or notary office before an agent can use it, adding an extra administrative step compared to signing directly in Egypt.

    A Tawkil Signed Abroad Needs Further Legalization

    A power of attorney signed and notarized in another country, such as at a notary public in the buyer's home country, must go through a chain of authentication before Egyptian institutions will accept it, typically including notarization, an apostille or foreign ministry certification, and Egyptian consular legalization.

    Countries that are members of the Hague Apostille Convention can often use a simplified apostille process, while others require full consular legalization at the Egyptian embassy, so the exact steps depend heavily on which country the document originates from.

    Most Foreign Property Purchases in Resort Areas Use Tawkil

    In Red Sea resort cities like Hurghada, a large share of property transactions involving foreign buyers rely on a tawkil model, where a special power of attorney is issued to the buyer or their representative after full payment, granting practical control over the unit even before a full title transfer.

    This approach developed partly because formal individual title registration for many resort developments has historically been slow or incomplete, so a notarized tawkil became the practical way for foreign buyers to secure usable rights over a specific unit while broader registration processes catch up.

    The Seller Must Be Present When Issuing a Property Tawkil

    When a tawkil is used to hand over practical control of a property in a sale, notarial practice generally requires the seller, the original owner, to appear in person at the notary office to sign, while the buyer or their appointed representative may not always need to attend personally.

    This requirement exists because the tawkil derives its authority from the seller's own legal standing over the property, so their presence and verified identity are what makes the resulting document valid, regardless of how the buyer chooses to be represented.

    A Tawkil Is Not the Same as a Full Property Title Transfer

    Holding a notarized tawkil over a property gives an agent or buyer significant practical control, including the ability to manage, rent, or resell it in many cases, but it does not by itself constitute full legal ownership registered in that person's name in the property registry.

    This distinction matters most when a tawkil holder eventually wants to sell to a third party or pass the property to heirs, since a clean chain of formal registered title generally provides stronger long-term legal certainty than relying on tawkil-based control indefinitely.

    A Tawkil Can Be Revoked by the Principal at Any Time

    The principal generally retains the right to revoke a tawkil before its purposes are completed, by formally cancelling it through the same notary office or a court declaration, which then needs to be communicated to any third party relying on the original document.

    Revocation becomes legally effective once properly registered and, in practice, once relevant parties like banks or government offices are notified, since an agent or counterparty who is unaware of the revocation and acts in good faith may still create complications that need separate legal resolution.

    Death of the Principal Automatically Ends the Tawkil

    Under Egyptian law, a power of attorney terminates automatically upon the death of the principal, since the agent's authority derives entirely from the principal's own legal capacity, which ceases to exist at that point regardless of what the original document says.

    This has real consequences for property held under a tawkil arrangement, since the deceased principal's estate then becomes subject to standard inheritance rules and procedures, and the tawkil holder cannot continue acting on the old authorization to manage or sell the property.

    Powers of Attorney Have an Optional Expiration Date

    A tawkil can be drafted either as open-ended, remaining valid until revoked, or with a specific expiration date or condition, such as being valid only for the duration of a single property transaction or for a fixed number of years.

    Lawyers commonly recommend attaching a clear expiration or purpose limitation to a tawkil whenever possible, since an open-ended general power of attorney left active indefinitely after its original purpose is complete is a recurring source of later disputes and misuse.

    A Court-Issued Tawkil Covers Litigation Representation

    A specific category of tawkil authorizes a lawyer to represent the principal in Egyptian court proceedings, distinct from a commercial or property tawkil, and is typically required by court rules before any lawyer can file pleadings or appear on a client's behalf.

    This litigation tawkil is usually narrower in scope, tied to a specific case number or legal matter, and Egyptian courts routinely check that a lawyer appearing for a party holds a valid, properly registered tawkil before accepting filings.

    Businesses Use Tawkil to Delegate Signing Authority

    Egyptian companies commonly issue an internal tawkil authorizing a specific manager or employee to sign contracts, open bank accounts, or represent the company before government bodies like the Tax Authority or the General Authority for Investment, without the company's owner needing to be personally present.

    This corporate use follows the same general-versus-special distinction, with companies typically preferring narrowly scoped tawkils for specific functions, such as banking authority separate from contract-signing authority, to limit any single employee's ability to bind the company broadly.

    Marriage and Divorce Tawkil Cases Follow Special Court Rules

    An Egyptian citizen abroad who cannot appear in person for a marriage registration or a divorce proceeding can, in specific circumstances, issue a tawkil authorizing a representative to complete the procedure, though family court rules impose additional scrutiny on these documents given their personal nature.

    Because personal-status matters directly affect legal rights like inheritance and custody, courts generally require stricter verification of a marriage or divorce tawkil's authenticity than a routine commercial power of attorney would receive.

    Fees for Issuing a Tawkil Are Modest but Add Up With Legalization

    The base notary fee for issuing a tawkil at an Egyptian notary office is relatively modest and set by official schedule, but the total cost rises when a property tawkil requires an accredited translator, or when a foreign-signed document needs apostille and consular legalization fees layered on top.

    Buyers and agents planning a property transaction abroad should budget for these layered costs and the time each legalization step takes, since underestimating the process has caused real delays in completing time-sensitive resort property purchases.

    A Tawkil Cannot Authorize Actions the Principal Could Not Do Themselves

    Egyptian law follows the general legal principle that an agent's authority under a tawkil cannot exceed what the principal themselves could lawfully do, meaning a tawkil cannot be used to bypass legal restrictions the principal is personally subject to, such as restrictions on land ownership in certain border or military zones.

    This limitation is relevant for foreign buyers specifically, since certain categories of Egyptian land carry ownership restrictions for non-Egyptians regardless of whether a local agent holds a broad tawkil, meaning the underlying legal restriction still applies to the transaction.

    Digital and Remote Notarization Has Expanded Slowly

    Egypt's Ministry of Justice has introduced some digital services for scheduling notary appointments and checking document status, but full remote or fully digital tawkil issuance without an in-person or consular appearance is not yet standard practice as of 2026.

    Most people needing to issue a tawkil, whether inside Egypt or through an embassy abroad, should still expect an in-person appointment as a required step, and should not assume a purely online process will be sufficient for a legally binding power of attorney.

    Disputes Over Agent Overreach Go to Civil Court

    If an agent acts beyond the scope of a tawkil, such as selling a property when only authorized to manage its rental, the principal can challenge the resulting transaction in civil court as exceeding the agent's actual authority, potentially voiding the unauthorized act.

    Third parties who dealt with the agent in good faith, relying on what appeared to be valid authority, sometimes retain some protection depending on the circumstances, which is why courts examine both the tawkil's exact wording and whether the third party could reasonably have known its limits.

    A Tawkil Should Name the Agent's Full Legal Identity

    Egyptian notarial practice requires the agent to be identified precisely by their full legal name and national ID or passport number, since a vaguely described agent, such as by nickname or partial name, can create disputes about exactly who was authorized to act.

    This precision matters especially when multiple family members share similar names, a common situation in Egypt, where an imprecisely worded tawkil has occasionally led to confusion or exploitation by someone other than the intended agent.

    Powers of Attorney for Vehicles Follow a Similar Pattern

    Selling or registering a car in Egypt on someone else's behalf, common when a family member is traveling or when a dealership handles paperwork for a buyer, also uses a tawkil, notarized similarly to property tawkils but referencing the vehicle's registration details instead of a property deed.

    Traffic authorities and licensing offices require this documented authority before processing an ownership transfer or registration renewal submitted by someone other than the vehicle's registered owner, mirroring the broader principle that Egyptian bureaucracy generally will not act on a third party's request without formal notarized proof of delegation.

    Scammers Sometimes Exploit Broad Tawkils With Elderly Relatives

    Egyptian legal aid organizations and family lawyers have documented cases where a broad general tawkil obtained from an elderly or vulnerable relative was later used to sell family property or drain bank accounts without the principal's full understanding of what they signed.

    This risk has led some lawyers to recommend that families avoid general tawkils for elderly relatives entirely, favoring narrowly scoped special tawkils reviewed by an independent lawyer, along with periodic check-ins to confirm the arrangement still reflects the principal's wishes.

    Checking a Tawkil's Validity Is Possible Through the Notary Registry

    Because every notarized tawkil is registered in the Real Estate Publicity Department's official records, a party relying on someone's claimed authority, such as a buyer dealing with a seller's agent, can verify the document's authenticity and current validity through that registry rather than trusting a photocopy alone.

    This verification step is a standard part of due diligence in Egyptian property transactions, since a tawkil that was already revoked or that has expired but is still being presented as valid is a documented source of fraud that registry checks are designed to catch.

    Banking Tawkils Let a Representative Manage an Account

    A bank-specific tawkil authorizes a named representative to conduct transactions on someone's account, such as withdrawals, deposits, or transfers, and Egyptian banks typically require their own additional documentation on top of the notarized tawkil before activating this arrangement internally.

    Banks generally treat this as a higher-risk service and often limit what a banking agent can do compared to the account holder themselves, such as capping withdrawal amounts or excluding the ability to close the account entirely, as an internal safeguard against misuse.

    Employment and Labor Matters Sometimes Require a Tawkil

    An employee unable to appear personally to sign a labor contract, collect an end-of-service settlement, or represent themselves before a labor office dispute can authorize a representative through a tawkil scoped specifically to that employment matter.

    Labor offices and the National Organization for Social Insurance generally require the same notarization standard as other tawkils, meaning an unnotarized authorization letter from an employee is typically insufficient for a representative to act on binding employment or insurance matters.

    A Tawkil Differs From a Simple Authorization Letter

    Many people confuse an informal authorization letter, sometimes used for low-stakes tasks like picking up a package or a school document, with a legally binding tawkil, but Egyptian institutions dealing with property, banking, or court matters will not accept the former as a substitute for the latter.

    The key distinguishing factor is notarization: an authorization letter is simply signed by the parties involved, while a tawkil is registered with an official notary office and carries the government's certification of its authenticity and the signatory's identity.

    A Renewed or Amended Tawkil Must Be Re-Notarized

    If circumstances change and a tawkil needs updated scope, a new expiration date, or a different named agent, the change cannot simply be handwritten onto the existing document; a fresh or amended tawkil must go through the same notarization process as the original.

    This requirement protects all parties by ensuring any change to an agent's authority is officially recorded and traceable, preventing disputes later over whether an informal amendment to an existing tawkil was ever actually valid.

    Divorced or Separated Spouses May Need to Coordinate Old Tawkils

    A tawkil granted by one spouse to the other during a marriage, such as authority to manage joint property or a shared business, does not automatically end upon separation or divorce unless it is formally revoked, which can create real complications if the relationship turns adversarial.

    Family lawyers commonly advise clients going through a divorce to review and formally revoke any standing tawkil given to the soon-to-be former spouse as an early step, well before the broader divorce and asset-division process concludes.

    Common Mistakes Include Vague Scope and Missing Expiration

    The most frequent problems lawyers see with Egyptian tawkils are overly vague descriptions of the authorized action, missing or overly distant expiration terms, and failure to specify the agent's full identifying details, all of which can be avoided with careful drafting before the notary appointment.

    A short checklist before signing, confirming the exact action authorized, the agent's full legal identity, an appropriate expiration or revocation plan, and translator arrangements if needed, catches most of the issues that later cause disputes or rejected paperwork.

    Working With a Local Lawyer Reduces Tawkil-Related Risk

    Given the interaction of general versus special scope, notarization requirements, translator rules, cross-border legalization, and revocation timing, most foreign buyers and residents dealing with a significant tawkil are advised to work through a licensed Egyptian lawyer rather than relying solely on a real estate agent or developer's in-house paperwork.

    A lawyer independent from the seller or developer can review the exact wording before signing, confirm the notary appointment and translator requirements are met, and verify the resulting document's registration, catching issues that are far harder to fix after the tawkil is already signed.

    FAQ

    What is the difference between a general and special tawkil in Egypt?

    A general tawkil grants broad authority across nearly any legal or financial matter, while a special tawkil limits the agent to specific, clearly named actions, such as selling one property.

    Where is a power of attorney notarized in Egypt?

    A tawkil is notarized at the Real Estate Publicity Department, known as Al Shahr Al Aqari, which operates under the Ministry of Justice through notary offices across the country.

    Do I need a translator to sign a tawkil in Egypt if I don't speak Arabic?

    Yes, Egyptian notary offices require a court-accredited translator to be present when the principal does not speak Arabic fluently, and will not certify the document without one.

    Can I issue a power of attorney for Egypt while living abroad?

    Yes, you can sign it at an Egyptian embassy or consulate abroad, or notarize it locally and then have it legalized through apostille or consular authentication before it is used in Egypt.

    Is a tawkil the same as owning a property in Egypt?

    No, a tawkil gives significant practical control but does not by itself constitute full legal title registered in the holder's name in the property registry.

    What happens to a tawkil if the principal dies?

    It terminates automatically, since the agent's authority depends entirely on the principal's legal capacity, which ends at death; the property or assets then fall under standard inheritance rules.

    Can a tawkil be cancelled?

    Yes, the principal can revoke it at any time through the same notary office or a court declaration, and should notify any third party relying on the original document.

    Why do so many foreign property buyers in Hurghada use tawkil?

    Because formal individual title registration in many resort developments has historically been slow, a notarized special tawkil became the practical way to secure usable control over a unit after full payment.

    Does the seller need to be present to issue a property tawkil?

    Yes, notarial practice generally requires the seller to appear in person since the tawkil derives its authority from the seller's own legal standing over the property.

    Can an agent under a tawkil do anything the document doesn't mention?

    No, an agent's authority is limited to what the tawkil explicitly states, and acting beyond that scope can be challenged in civil court as unauthorized.

    How can I verify a tawkil is genuine and still valid?

    Since every notarized tawkil is registered with the Real Estate Publicity Department, you can verify its authenticity and current status through that registry rather than relying on a photocopy.

    Is a general tawkil risky to give to a family member?

    It can be, since it grants sweeping authority; lawyers generally recommend a narrowly scoped special tawkil for a defined task instead, especially for elderly or vulnerable principals.

    Does a tawkil expire automatically?

    Only if it is drafted with a specific expiration date or a limited purpose; otherwise it remains open-ended and valid until formally revoked or the principal dies.

    Can a tawkil authorize something illegal for the principal to do?

    No, an agent's authority under a tawkil cannot exceed what the principal could lawfully do themselves, so it cannot bypass legal restrictions like foreign land-ownership limits.

    Should I use a lawyer when signing a tawkil in Egypt?

    Yes, given the legal complexity of scope, notarization, translation, and cross-border legalization rules, a licensed independent lawyer is strongly recommended, especially for property transactions.
