    • Saudi Arabia Splits Emergencies Into Separate Numbers

    Unlike countries with one unified emergency number, Saudi Arabia traditionally assigns a separate line to each emergency service: police, ambulance and civil defense each have their own dedicated number rather than routing every call through a single operator.

    This structure means knowing which number matches which emergency actually matters in Saudi Arabia, more so than in places where a single number automatically routes the caller to the right responder based on the nature of the emergency.

    997 Is the Number for Ambulance Service

    Dial 997 to reach the Saudi Red Crescent Authority for ambulance and emergency medical response anywhere in the Kingdom. This is the number to call for medical emergencies, serious injuries, or any situation requiring urgent paramedic response.

    The Red Crescent operates the national ambulance network and coordinates with hospitals to route patients to the nearest appropriate facility, so callers should be ready to describe the medical situation and exact location clearly.

    998 Reaches Saudi Civil Defense for Fires

    Dial 998 for the Saudi Civil Defense, the agency responsible for firefighting, fire safety inspections, and response to building collapses, chemical spills, and other structural emergencies across the country.

    Civil Defense also handles broader disaster response coordination during floods, sandstorm-related incidents and other events beyond fire alone, making 998 the right call for structural or environmental hazards, not just active blazes.

    999 Connects to the Police Force Nationwide

    Dial 999 for the general police force to report crimes, request police assistance, or seek help with a public safety matter. This number functions across the Kingdom as the standard police contact line, distinct from the ambulance and civil defense numbers.

    For anything involving theft, an active threat, a dispute requiring law enforcement, or a road accident with injuries requiring police documentation, 999 is generally the correct number to dial regardless of which specific city or region you are in.

    911 Is a Unified Line in Three Major Regions

    The 911 unified security operations number currently operates in the Makkah, Riyadh and Eastern Province regions, letting callers in those specific areas reach a combined dispatch center covering police, ambulance and civil defense coordination through one number.

    Outside these three regions, 911 may not connect to the same unified service, so travelers moving between regions should default to the separate 997, 998 and 999 numbers unless they know they are within one of the covered zones.

    The 911 Rollout Reflects a Gradual Modernization

    Saudi Arabia has been gradually expanding its unified 911 emergency dispatch model beyond its original launch regions, following the pattern used by many countries that consolidate emergency response into a single number over time rather than all at once.

    Until nationwide rollout is complete, residents and visitors should not assume 911 works identically everywhere in the country, and should keep the separate service-specific numbers on hand as a reliable fallback in non-covered regions.

    Traffic Police Have Their Own Dedicated Number

    Dial 993 to reach the Traffic Police Force for matters specifically related to road traffic enforcement and accident reporting within city limits, distinct from the general 999 police line used for broader law enforcement concerns.

    For minor accidents without injuries, many drivers use the Ministry of Interior's Najm platform or app to document the incident digitally instead of waiting for an officer, though serious accidents should still involve a direct call to police or traffic authorities.

    Highway Patrol Uses a Separate Line From City Traffic Police

    Dial 996 for the Highway Traffic Police Force, which handles incidents and enforcement on intercity highways and major roads outside urban centers, as opposed to the 993 line used for traffic matters within a city.

    This distinction matters for long-distance drivers, since an incident on a desert highway between cities is handled through a different dispatch structure than a fender-bender on a city street, even though both ultimately involve traffic police.

    Electricity Emergencies Have Their Own Hotline

    Dial 933 to reach Saudi Electricity Company customer service for power outages, downed lines, or other electrical emergencies, a number separate from the core police, ambulance and fire lines but still treated as urgent for safety reasons.

    A downed power line or exposed wiring is a genuine safety hazard, and calling 933 promptly, along with keeping people away from the area, is the appropriate first response rather than waiting for it to be noticed by someone else.

    Water and Sewage Issues Route to a Regional Line

    Dial 939 for water and sewage service issues in the Eastern Province, one of several regionally organized utility hotlines in Saudi Arabia; other regions may use different numbers or a national water authority contact, so checking the local number matters.

    Because utility services in Saudi Arabia are sometimes organized regionally rather than through one single national hotline, residents moving to a new city should confirm the correct local number rather than assuming the same digits apply everywhere.

    Municipal Services Handle Non-Emergency City Issues

    Dial 940 for municipal services covering issues like street maintenance, waste collection problems, and other city infrastructure concerns that are not urgent emergencies but still require official attention from local authorities.

    This number is a useful middle ground between doing nothing about a persistent local problem and escalating it as an emergency call, giving residents a proper channel for issues like a broken streetlight or an uncollected garbage complaint.

    Natural Disaster Response Has a National Hotline

    Dial 966 for coordination during natural disasters such as major flooding or severe weather events, which typically triggers a broader multi-agency response beyond what a single fire, police, or ambulance call would mobilize on its own.

    During significant weather events, Saudi authorities also issue public warnings via official channels and apps, so this number works alongside, not instead of, monitoring official alerts during storm or flood-prone periods of the year.

    The Ministry of Health Runs a Separate Health Hotline

    Dial 937 for the Ministry of Health's non-emergency health information and services line, useful for medical questions, appointment guidance, and general health inquiries that do not require dispatching an ambulance through 997.

    This distinction between 937 for health information and 997 for active medical emergencies helps route calls appropriately, keeping ambulance dispatchers free to focus on genuine emergencies rather than general health questions.

    Anti-Narcotics Reports Have a Dedicated Confidential Line

    Dial 995 to report drug-related activity to the Anti-Narcotics division, a confidential reporting line separate from general police, intended specifically for tips about drug trafficking, dealing, or related criminal activity.

    This specialized line allows the anti-narcotics unit to handle sensitive tips with appropriate discretion, rather than routing drug-related reports through the general police line where the specific expertise needed might not be immediately available.

    Border and Passport Matters Use Separate Government Lines

    Dial 994 for border checkpoint matters and 992 for passport-related services, both handled by immigration authorities separately from the emergency response numbers, covering administrative issues rather than urgent safety situations.

    Travelers dealing with a passport problem or a border-crossing question should use these dedicated lines rather than calling 999, since police dispatch is not the right channel for administrative immigration paperwork issues.

    General Security Concerns Have a Reporting Line

    Dial 989 for Public Security matters and 990 for telephone reporting of broader security issues, both distinct channels intended for concerns that relate to state security or public order rather than an immediate crime in progress.

    These lines exist alongside 999 to give the public a way to report concerns that may not require an immediate police dispatch but still warrant attention from security authorities, such as suspicious activity observed over time.

    Every Number Works Free From Any Phone

    All of Saudi Arabia's emergency numbers are free to dial from any landline or mobile phone, including from a phone with no active SIM card credit or from a locked handset, similar to emergency-calling requirements in most countries worldwide.

    This means a dead prepaid balance or an expired SIM should never prevent someone from reaching emergency services; the call will still connect through the network's mandatory emergency-access provisions regardless of account status.

    Operators Generally Speak Arabic With Some English Support

    Emergency dispatch calls are handled primarily in Arabic, though major city dispatch centers, particularly in Riyadh, Jeddah and the Eastern Province, often have staff who can communicate in basic English for non-Arabic-speaking callers.

    Non-Arabic speakers should speak slowly, state the emergency type and exact location clearly, and consider having a nearby Arabic speaker assist if one is available, since language barriers can meaningfully slow response coordination.

    State Your Exact Location First and Clearly

    Emergency dispatchers rely on the caller to describe their location accurately, ideally including a landmark, building name, street name and city, since not every location in Saudi Arabia has GPS-precise addressing that a dispatcher can instantly pull up.

    Apps like the National Address service or a maps app showing coordinates can supplement a verbal description, and providing this information early in the call, before details of the emergency itself, speeds up dispatching a responder to the right place.

    Stay on the Line Until the Dispatcher Ends the Call

    Hanging up immediately after giving basic information can mean missing follow-up questions the dispatcher needs answered, such as whether the situation is changing, how many people are involved, or specific access instructions for reaching the location.

    Dispatchers typically end the call themselves once they have enough information and the appropriate unit has been dispatched, so staying on the line, even briefly, is a small habit that meaningfully improves how well responders are prepared on arrival.

    Non-Emergency Issues Should Avoid Emergency Lines

    Calling 999, 998 or 997 for a non-urgent matter ties up lines and dispatcher time that genuine emergencies need. Non-urgent police, fire safety, or medical questions have their own separate channels, such as 937 for general health information.

    This is not merely a courtesy; overloading emergency lines with non-critical calls can genuinely slow response for someone else facing a real emergency at the same time, which is why each service publishes a distinct non-emergency contact where possible.

    Hospitals Distinguish Between Government and Private Care

    In a serious emergency, ambulances dispatched via 997 generally take patients to the nearest appropriate government hospital, while patients who prefer a specific private hospital may need to arrange separate transport if their condition allows for it safely.

    Foreign residents and tourists without Saudi health insurance should be aware that emergency treatment is typically provided regardless of insurance status, but billing and follow-up costs at government versus private facilities can differ significantly.

    Hajj and Umrah Seasons Add Special-Purpose Response Units

    During the Hajj and Umrah seasons, additional temporary medical, fire and security response units and field hospitals are deployed around Mecca and Medina to handle the enormous surge in population density during pilgrimage periods.

    Pilgrims are generally advised to look for the nearest visible Red Crescent or Civil Defense post within the crowd rather than trying to make a phone call in a very dense, loud environment where hearing a dispatcher clearly can be difficult.

    Domestic Violence and Abuse Have Confidential Reporting Channels

    The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development, along with dedicated domestic violence hotlines, offer confidential channels for reporting abuse separate from the general 999 police line, aimed at ensuring victims are connected with appropriate social and legal support.

    These specialized channels reflect a recognition that survivors of abuse may need a different kind of initial response than a standard police dispatch, including social workers trained specifically for these sensitive situations.

    Mobile Carriers Publish Their Own Customer Emergency Guides

    STC, Mobily and Zain each publish guidance for customers on which emergency numbers to use and how to reach services if a phone lacks signal from the primary carrier but can still access another network's emergency broadcast.

    Most modern phones can dial an emergency number even using a different carrier's signal if the primary network is unavailable, a safety feature mandated across telecom networks in Saudi Arabia as in most countries.

    Save Numbers Before You Need Them, Not During

    Programming 997, 998, 999 and the 911 unified line, where applicable, into a phone's contacts before an emergency happens saves critical seconds compared with searching for the right number while already in a stressful, urgent situation.

    Many residents also keep a printed card with these numbers in a car glovebox or a home's visible location, which is useful in situations where a phone is lost, damaged, or out of battery during the emergency itself.

    False Emergency Reports Carry Legal Consequences

    Deliberately making a false report to emergency services, such as a fake bomb threat or a fabricated crime report, is treated as a criminal offense in Saudi Arabia, since it diverts limited emergency resources away from genuine incidents.

    This applies even to reports made as a prank, and authorities can trace calls back to the originating number, meaning a false report is not a low-risk act even if the caller believes it will not be taken seriously.

    Tourist Areas Have Rapid Response Units on Standby

    Major tourist destinations such as AlUla, the Red Sea coast resorts, and central Riyadh and Jeddah entertainment districts typically maintain a visible security and medical presence, with faster response times than more remote areas of the country.

    Travelers in remote desert or mountain areas farther from major cities should recognize that response times for any of the emergency numbers may be considerably longer, and plan trips to such areas with that reality in mind.

    The Najm App Digitizes Minor Accident Reporting

    For minor traffic accidents without injuries, Saudi Arabia's Najm platform, run through insurance companies with government backing, lets both drivers document the accident digitally and generate an official report without waiting for police to physically arrive.

    This system was introduced specifically to reduce traffic congestion caused by drivers leaving damaged vehicles in place waiting for a police report, and it has become the standard first step for accidents that do not involve injury or dispute.

    Consulates Supplement, but Do Not Replace, Local Emergency Services

    Foreign embassies and consulates in Saudi Arabia can assist their citizens with documentation, family notification and legal referrals after an emergency, but they are not a substitute for calling the actual Saudi emergency number during the incident itself.

    Travelers should call 997, 998 or 999 first for any active emergency, then contact their embassy afterward for consular support such as replacing a lost passport or helping coordinate with family members back home.

    Emergency Numbers Are Free of Charge Even While Roaming

    International visitors using a foreign SIM card with roaming enabled can still dial Saudi emergency numbers free of charge, since emergency-access rules apply to any device connecting to a Saudi mobile network regardless of the SIM's country of origin.

    This means a traveler does not need a local Saudi SIM specifically to reach emergency services, though having one for general connectivity and easier callback communication with dispatchers is still generally recommended for longer stays.

    Verify Current Numbers Through Official Channels Before Traveling

    Because Saudi Arabia continues rolling out the unified 911 system regionally and periodically updates service hotlines, travelers and new residents should confirm current numbers through my.gov.sa or their mobile carrier rather than relying only on older printed guides.

    A number correct for one city or one year is not guaranteed to be identical everywhere or to stay unchanged indefinitely, so treating official government sources as the primary reference is the safest approach for accuracy.

    FAQ

    What number do I call for an ambulance in Saudi Arabia?

    Dial 997 to reach the Saudi Red Crescent Authority for ambulance and emergency medical response anywhere in the Kingdom.

    What number is for police in Saudi Arabia?

    Dial 999 for the general police force nationwide. In the Makkah, Riyadh and Eastern Province regions, 911 also connects to a unified dispatch center covering police alongside ambulance and civil defense.

    What number do I call for a fire?

    Dial 998 to reach Saudi Civil Defense, which handles firefighting as well as building collapses, chemical spills and other structural emergencies.

    Does 911 work everywhere in Saudi Arabia?

    No. The unified 911 line currently operates only in the Makkah, Riyadh and Eastern Province regions. Elsewhere, use the separate 997, 998 and 999 numbers.

    Are emergency calls free in Saudi Arabia?

    Yes. All emergency numbers are free to dial from any phone, including one with no active SIM credit, and this applies even to visitors using international roaming.

    What number handles traffic accidents?

    For city traffic matters and accident reporting, dial 993. For accidents on intercity highways, dial 996 for the Highway Traffic Police. Minor accidents without injuries can often be documented through the Najm app instead.

    How do I report a power outage or downed line?

    Dial 933 to reach Saudi Electricity Company customer service for outages, downed lines and other electrical emergencies.

    Is there a non-emergency health information line?

    Yes. Dial 937 for the Ministry of Health's general health information and services line, which is separate from the 997 ambulance dispatch number.

    How do I report drug-related activity?

    Dial 995 for a confidential line to the Anti-Narcotics division, separate from the general police number.

    Will emergency operators speak English?

    Dispatch is primarily in Arabic, but major city centers, especially in Riyadh, Jeddah and the Eastern Province, often have staff with basic English. Speak slowly and state the location and emergency type clearly.

    What should I say first when calling an emergency number?

    State your exact location first, ideally with a landmark, building name and street, then describe the nature of the emergency, and stay on the line until the dispatcher ends the call.

    Can I get in trouble for a false emergency report?

    Yes. Deliberately making a false report, including as a prank, is a criminal offense in Saudi Arabia, and authorities can trace the call back to the caller.

    Where do I report domestic violence confidentially?

    The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development and dedicated domestic violence hotlines offer confidential reporting channels separate from the general 999 police line.

    Should I call my embassy during an emergency instead of local services?

    No. Call the Saudi emergency number first for any active emergency, then contact your embassy afterward for consular support like document replacement or family notification.

    What number do I use for a minor fender-bender with no injuries?

    Many drivers document minor accidents without injuries through the Najm app or platform instead of waiting for police, which speeds up clearing the vehicles and generates an official report digitally.
