UAE Vehicle Registration (Mulkiya) Guide

    • The Mulkiya Is the Vehicle's Legal Identity Document

    The Mulkiya is the official vehicle registration card issued by an emirate's roads and transport authority. It records the vehicle's chassis number, engine details, ownership, plate number, and registration expiry date in one document.

    Driving a vehicle without a valid Mulkiya, or with an expired one, is a traffic offense in every emirate, since police and toll systems cross-check plate numbers against active registration records in real time.

    Registration Is Valid for One Year at a Time

    Under UAE traffic law, vehicle registration is issued for a maximum of twelve months regardless of emirate. There is no multi-year registration option for private vehicles, so renewal is an annual, recurring administrative task for every owner.

    The exact validity end date is printed on the Mulkiya itself, and owners are expected to track it themselves, since reminders from the roads authority via SMS or app notification are a courtesy rather than a legal substitute for tracking the date.

    Renewal Can Start Well Before Expiry

    Owners in Dubai can begin the renewal process up to 150 days before the registration's expiry date, giving ample flexibility to complete inspection, insurance renewal, and payment without rushing near the deadline.

    Renewing early does not shorten the next registration period. The new twelve-month validity starts from the original expiry date or the renewal date depending on the system, so early renewal is purely a convenience, not a loss.

    Vehicle Inspection Is Required Once a Car Reaches Three Years

    New vehicles are generally exempt from mandatory inspection for their first three years of registration. From the third year onward, a physical inspection at an approved testing center is required before each annual renewal.

    The inspection checks brakes, tires, lights, emissions, chassis condition, and other safety systems. A vehicle that fails must be repaired and re-tested before registration can be renewed, and a re-inspection fee applies.

    Valid Insurance Is a Prerequisite, Not an Afterthought

    Renewal cannot be completed without an active, valid motor insurance policy registered against the vehicle's plate number. The system checks insurance validity automatically before allowing the renewal payment to proceed.

    This means insurance renewal needs to be arranged first, either directly with an insurer or through a broker, with enough lead time that the policy is active in the system before attempting to renew the Mulkiya.

    Outstanding Traffic Fines Block Renewal

    Any unpaid traffic fines registered against the vehicle or its owner must be cleared before the roads authority will process a Mulkiya renewal. The system checks this automatically at the point of payment.

    Owners can check their fine status through the relevant emirate's traffic portal or app before starting renewal, avoiding the surprise of a blocked transaction partway through what should be a quick process.

    Renewal Fees Are Standardized by Vehicle Category

    In Dubai, a standard light private vehicle renewal costs AED 350, plus a flat AED 20 Knowledge and Innovation fee, bringing the typical total to AED 370. Commercial and heavier vehicle categories carry different fee tiers.

    These figures apply as of the current fee schedule and are subject to periodic revision by the roads authority, so owners should confirm the exact amount on the official renewal portal at the time of payment.

    Vehicle Testing Adds a Separate Fee

    For vehicles requiring inspection, the testing center fee is charged separately from the registration renewal fee, typically running roughly AED 150 to 170 depending on vehicle type and testing center.

    A failed inspection that needs a re-test adds an additional charge on top of the original testing fee, along with the standard AED 20 Knowledge and Innovation fee applied to most government transactions.

    Renewal Can Be Completed Fully Online

    Owners can renew registration through the roads authority's official website, its dedicated mobile app, or in Dubai through the Mahboub chatbot, without visiting a service center if insurance and inspection are already valid.

    Login is typically done using UAE Pass, an Emirates ID-linked digital identity, or a driving license and plate number combination, after which the system pulls up the vehicle's record for renewal.

    A New Mulkiya Is Issued Digitally by Default

    Most emirates now issue a digital Mulkiya accessible through the roads authority's app immediately after payment confirmation, meaning drivers can show it on their phone during a traffic stop without a physical card.

    A physical card can still be requested for a delivery fee, with standard, same-day, and express delivery options offered at different price points for owners who prefer or need the printed version.

    Late Renewal Triggers a Monthly Penalty

    Failing to renew registration by the expiry date generally results in a fine of AED 20 per month of delay, applied automatically and added to the eventual renewal transaction once the owner returns to complete it.

    This penalty accrues independently of whether the vehicle was actually driven during the lapse, since the fine is tied to the registration status itself rather than usage or mileage recorded during the gap.

    Extended Delays Escalate to a Larger Fine and Black Points

    If renewal is delayed beyond roughly three months past expiry, the penalty structure escalates significantly, commonly to a flat AED 500 fine plus four black points recorded against the vehicle owner's driving record.

    Black points can affect a driver's license standing over time, including potential suspension if enough points accumulate across various violations, making a long-lapsed registration a more serious issue than a simple late fee.

    Ownership Transfer Uses the Same Registration System

    Selling or buying a used vehicle in the UAE requires transferring registration through the same roads authority system used for renewal, updating the Mulkiya to reflect the new owner's name and Emirates ID.

    Both buyer and seller typically need to be present, or act through a documented power of attorney, and any outstanding fines or loans against the vehicle must be settled before the transfer can be completed.

    Registration Category Depends on Vehicle Use, Not Just Type

    The roads authority classifies vehicles as private, commercial, taxi, or public transport for registration purposes, and each category carries different fee schedules, inspection frequency, and insurance requirements beyond just engine size or body style.

    A vehicle used for ride-hailing or delivery work, for instance, generally needs to be registered under a commercial or public transport category even if it is a standard passenger car model.

    Salik and Toll Balances Need to Be Clear Too

    In Dubai, an outstanding negative balance or unpaid usage fees on the Salik road toll account linked to a vehicle can also block Mulkiya renewal, similar to how unpaid traffic fines do.

    Owners should check their toll account balance alongside fines before starting renewal, especially if the vehicle has changed hands or the toll tag was recently transferred between owners.

    Imported and Classic Vehicles Face Extra Registration Steps

    Vehicles imported privately from outside the UAE, or classic and modified vehicles, often go through an additional technical compliance check beyond the standard inspection before they can be registered or have registration renewed.

    This can include verifying the vehicle meets UAE specification standards, since some markets sell versions of the same model with different safety or emissions equipment than what is approved for local roads.

    Company-Registered Vehicles Follow the Same Cycle With More Paperwork

    Fleet or company-owned vehicles follow the same annual renewal cycle and inspection rules as private vehicles, but typically require the trade license and an authorized signatory's documentation for each transaction rather than an individual's ID alone.

    Businesses managing multiple vehicles often use a dedicated fleet account with the roads authority to batch-track renewal dates and reduce the risk of any single vehicle's registration lapsing unnoticed.

    Number Plates Are Registered Separately From the Vehicle Itself

    A UAE plate number, particularly a personalized or premium plate, is treated as a distinct asset from the vehicle it is mounted on, and can be transferred to a different vehicle or sold separately under its own process.

    This distinction matters when selling a car, since an owner with a valuable plate number may want to reserve it for a replacement vehicle rather than letting it transfer automatically to the buyer.

    Insurance Category Choice Affects Renewal Smoothness

    Comprehensive insurance policies generally sync more reliably with the roads authority's renewal system than some third-party-only policies from smaller insurers, since major insurers have direct data integration with the authority.

    If a renewal attempt fails to recognize a valid policy, contacting the insurer to confirm the policy was correctly registered in the shared system is usually faster than assuming the roads authority made an error.

    Each Emirate Runs Its Own Roads Authority System

    Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority, Abu Dhabi's Department of Municipalities and Transport, and equivalent bodies in Sharjah and other emirates each operate their own registration systems, with broadly similar but not identical fees and procedures.

    A vehicle registered in one emirate can generally be driven throughout the UAE, but transferring its registration to a different emirate if the owner relocates involves its own separate transfer process.

    Renewal Reminders Are Sent but Not Guaranteed

    Most roads authorities send SMS or app push notifications ahead of a vehicle's registration expiry as a courtesy, but the legal responsibility to renew on time rests entirely with the owner regardless of whether a reminder arrived.

    Owners who change their registered phone number without updating it with the authority risk missing these reminders entirely, so keeping contact details current in the system is a simple way to avoid accidental lapses.

    A Roadworthy Test Failure Report Details Needed Repairs

    When a vehicle fails inspection, the testing center issues a report listing the specific faults found, whether brakes, suspension, lights, or emissions, which the owner must have repaired before returning for re-testing.

    Keeping repair invoices can be useful if a dispute arises over whether a fix was properly completed, and some testing centers allow uploading proof of repair as part of the re-inspection booking.

    Fleet and Rental Vehicles Face More Frequent Checks

    Vehicles used commercially for rental or ride-hailing often face more frequent inspection requirements than standard private cars, reflecting their higher mileage and passenger-carrying use, regardless of the vehicle's age.

    Rental companies typically manage this compliance internally as part of fleet maintenance schedules, but individuals renting out a personal vehicle through a peer-to-peer platform need to check whether the same rules apply to them.

    A Suspended Registration Differs From a Cancelled One

    A registration can be temporarily suspended, for instance while a vehicle is exported, stored long-term, or undergoing a legal dispute, without fully cancelling the record, allowing reactivation later without starting registration from scratch.

    Cancelling registration entirely, by contrast, is typically done when a vehicle is permanently exported, scrapped, or removed from the road, and requires a formal deregistration process through the roads authority.

    Electric and Hybrid Vehicles Follow the Same Renewal Cycle

    Electric and hybrid vehicles are registered and renewed under the same annual Mulkiya cycle as conventional cars, with the same fee structure applying in most emirates, though some jurisdictions offer separate incentives elsewhere.

    The inspection checklist for electric vehicles is adapted to check battery and charging system condition rather than emissions, since traditional exhaust emissions testing does not apply to a vehicle with no tailpipe.

    Renewal Records Feed Into a Vehicle's History Report

    A consistent, unbroken renewal and inspection history contributes to a cleaner vehicle history report, which matters when selling a used car since buyers and dealers can check this record through the roads authority or approved services.

    Gaps in registration history, or repeated late renewals, can appear on such reports and may raise questions from a prospective buyer even if the vehicle itself is mechanically sound.

    Third-Party Renewal Services Add Convenience for a Fee

    Garages, insurance brokers, and typing centers commonly offer a full renewal service, handling inspection booking, insurance liaison, and payment on the owner's behalf for a service charge on top of official fees.

    This can be useful for owners with limited time or unfamiliarity with the online system, but the same renewal can be completed directly by the owner at no extra cost through the official app or website.

    Leased and Financed Vehicles Add a Lienholder to the Record

    A vehicle purchased through a bank loan or leased through a finance company typically shows the lender or leasing company as a noted interest on the Mulkiya until the loan is fully settled or the lease ends.

    Renewing registration on a financed vehicle generally proceeds the same as any other renewal, but selling or transferring it before the loan is cleared requires the lender's release, adding a step beyond standard ownership transfer.

    A Duplicate Mulkiya Can Be Requested if the Original Is Lost

    An owner who loses or damages their physical Mulkiya card can request a replacement through the roads authority's app or a service center, generally for a modest reissuance fee separate from the annual renewal charge.

    Since most emirates now issue a digital Mulkiya by default, a lost physical card is often less disruptive than in the past, as the digital version in the authority's app remains valid for identification purposes.

    Registration Renewal Is Distinct From Driving License Renewal

    A vehicle's Mulkiya renewal and the owner's personal driving license renewal are two separate processes with independent expiry dates, fees, and requirements, even though both are managed by the same roads authority.

    Owners sometimes confuse the two and assume renewing one automatically covers the other, but each must be tracked and renewed on its own schedule to avoid a lapse in either document.

    Registration Address Details Should Match Current Residency

    The Mulkiya record links to the owner's Emirates ID and registered contact details, so an owner who moves residence or changes their mobile number should update these details with the roads authority to keep renewal reminders and correspondence accurate.

    Outdated contact information does not itself block renewal, but it does increase the chance of missing a reminder or a request for additional documents during a transaction, adding avoidable delay.

    Checking the Official Portal Confirms Current Fees Before Paying

    Because fees, inspection rules, and digital service options are periodically updated by each emirate's roads authority, the most reliable step before renewal is checking the official portal or app for the current fee schedule.

    Third-party guides and blog posts, including this one, are useful for understanding the process, but the authority's own published fee list at the time of payment should always be treated as the final figure.

    FAQ

    What is a Mulkiya?

    The Mulkiya is the UAE's official vehicle registration card, showing the plate number, chassis and engine details, owner information, and registration expiry date.

    How much does Mulkiya renewal cost in Dubai?

    A standard light private vehicle renewal costs AED 350 plus a flat AED 20 Knowledge and Innovation fee, for a typical total of AED 370, though fees vary by vehicle category.

    When does a car need a physical inspection?

    Vehicles generally need inspection once they reach three years of age, and then before every annual renewal from that point onward.

    How early can I renew my vehicle registration?

    In Dubai, renewal can start up to 150 days before the expiry date, and early renewal does not shorten the next registration period.

    What happens if I renew my registration late?

    A late renewal generally incurs a fine of AED 20 per month of delay. Delays beyond about three months can escalate to a AED 500 fine plus four black points.

    Can I renew my Mulkiya without visiting a service center?

    Yes, renewal is typically available fully online through the roads authority's website or app, provided insurance is valid, inspection is up to date, and there are no outstanding fines.

    Do outstanding traffic fines block renewal?

    Yes, any unpaid traffic fines registered against the vehicle or owner must be cleared before the renewal transaction can be completed.

    Is a physical Mulkiya card still issued?

    A digital Mulkiya is issued by default and accessible via the roads authority app. A physical card can still be requested for an additional delivery fee.

    Does Salik or toll debt affect renewal?

    In Dubai, an outstanding negative balance on the Salik toll account linked to the vehicle can block Mulkiya renewal, similar to unpaid traffic fines.

    What documents are needed to transfer vehicle ownership?

    Both buyer and seller generally need valid Emirates IDs, and any outstanding fines or loans against the vehicle must be settled, before the roads authority can update the registration.

    Do electric vehicles need a different renewal process?

    No, electric and hybrid vehicles follow the same annual renewal cycle and fee structure, though the inspection checklist checks battery and charging system health instead of emissions.

    Can someone else renew my vehicle registration for me?

    Yes, garages, brokers, and typing centers commonly offer renewal services on an owner's behalf for a service fee, though the owner can also complete it directly at no extra cost.

    What happens if my vehicle fails inspection?

    The testing center issues a report listing the faults found, which must be repaired before the vehicle is re-tested, and a re-inspection fee applies to the second test.

    Is registration renewal the same across all emirates?

    The process is broadly similar, but each emirate's roads authority operates its own system with its own fee schedule and procedures, so amounts can differ slightly between emirates.

    What happens to the Mulkiya if my car loan is not fully paid?

    The lender is typically noted as an interested party on the Mulkiya until the loan is settled, and selling or transferring the vehicle before then requires the lender's formal release.
