    • The Ministry of Commerce Enforces Consumer Protection Law

    The Ministry of Commerce is the primary government body responsible for enforcing consumer protection regulations in Saudi Arabia, covering everything from defective goods to deceptive advertising and unfair pricing practices across retail and e-commerce.

    The ministry can investigate complaints, fine businesses, order refunds, and in serious or repeat cases suspend a merchant's commercial registration, giving it real enforcement power rather than just an advisory role over the retail sector.

    The 1900 Hotline Handles Complaints Around the Clock

    The Ministry of Commerce operates a toll-free hotline at 1900, available twenty-four hours a day, for consumers to report commercial violations, file complaints about a business, or ask questions about their rights under Saudi consumer law.

    Beyond phone calls, complaints can also be submitted through the ministry's official website or its social media accounts, giving consumers multiple channels to reach the same enforcement body depending on what is most convenient.

    The National Consumer Protection Association Advocates Separately

    Alongside the government's own enforcement channel, the National Consumer Protection Association, known as NCPA, is a government-supported advocacy body that offers free advice, mediation between consumers and businesses, and legal referrals.

    The NCPA can intervene informally on a consumer's behalf, often resolving a dispute faster than a formal ministry complaint process, making it a useful first stop before escalating a serious or unresolved issue to a formal regulatory complaint.

    Defective Goods Entitle Buyers to Replacement or Refund

    A consumer who buys an item that turns out to be defective, adulterated or counterfeit has the legal right to return it and claim a refund of the full purchase amount, rather than being limited to store credit or a partial exchange.

    Consumers are also entitled to free repair when the defect is fixable, and to compensation when a defect cannot be repaired or persists after repair within the applicable warranty period, giving several remedy paths depending on the situation.

    Online Purchases Carry a Seven-Day Return Right

    Under the E-Commerce Law, consumers can return most products bought online within seven days of the contract date, without needing a specific reason, as long as the product remains unused and has not had its benefit extracted.

    This cooling-off style right gives online shoppers a safety net similar to what many other countries provide for remote or distance purchases, addressing the fact that buyers cannot physically inspect an item before ordering it online.

    Some Product Categories Are Excluded From the Return Right

    The seven-day online return right does not apply universally; it excludes items like newspapers and magazines, downloaded software, CDs and DVDs, custom-made products built to a specific consumer's specifications, and transportation or catering services.

    These exceptions follow a common logic seen in many countries' consumer laws: goods that lose their resale value once accessed, or services already delivered and consumed, generally cannot be returned in the same way as an unused physical product.

    Delayed Shipments Beyond Fifteen Days Allow Cancellation

    If an online order is delayed by more than fifteen days beyond the promised delivery window, the consumer has the right to cancel the order and receive a full refund, rather than being forced to wait indefinitely for an item that never arrives.

    Online stores are also required to proactively inform buyers of shipping delays and specify a revised delivery deadline, rather than leaving the customer to guess at order status with no communication from the seller.

    Buyers Get Twenty-Four Hours to Fix an Order Mistake

    If a consumer notices an error in an online order right after placing it, such as the wrong size or quantity, they have twenty-four hours to correct the mistake through the store's official channels before the order enters standard processing.

    This window recognizes that mistakes happen quickly during checkout and gives buyers a short, defined grace period to fix an error without needing to go through a full formal return process after the item has already shipped.

    The E-Commerce Law Applies to Registered Online Stores

    Saudi Arabia's E-Commerce Law, issued in 2019 with implementing regulations effective from 2020, requires online sellers to be properly registered and to clearly disclose business information, pricing, and terms of sale to consumers before purchase.

    This registration requirement is tied to the Maroof platform, which verifies legitimate online stores, giving consumers a way to check whether a seller is officially recognized before trusting them with a purchase or payment details.

    Checking Maroof Verification Helps Spot Fake Stores

    Before buying from an unfamiliar online store, consumers can check for a Maroof verification badge, which confirms the store has registered with the Ministry of Commerce and provided verifiable business details, reducing the risk of dealing with a fraudulent seller.

    A store lacking this verification is not automatically illegitimate, but the badge gives an extra layer of confidence, and its absence is a reasonable prompt to research a seller further before sending payment for an unfamiliar product.

    Warranty Periods Set Minimum Manufacturer Obligations

    Products sold in Saudi Arabia typically come with a manufacturer or retailer warranty period during which repair, replacement or refund obligations apply for defects, and consumer law sets baseline expectations for how sellers must honor these commitments.

    Consumers should keep purchase receipts and warranty documentation, since a claim without proof of purchase date and terms is significantly harder to enforce, even when the underlying legal right to a remedy clearly exists.

    Deceptive Advertising Claims Can Be Formally Reported

    Advertising that makes false or misleading claims about a product's features, price, origin or effectiveness can be reported to the Ministry of Commerce, which has the authority to fine businesses and require corrective disclosure for violations.

    This covers both traditional advertising and increasingly common influencer marketing, where undisclosed sponsorships or exaggerated product claims made on social media can also fall within the scope of consumer protection enforcement.

    Price Gouging During Shortages Is Actively Monitored

    The Ministry of Commerce monitors markets for unjustified price increases during shortages or high-demand periods, such as around Ramadan or following a supply disruption, and can penalize merchants found to be exploiting such conditions unfairly.

    This monitoring has become more visible during specific events, like sudden demand spikes for certain goods, where authorities have publicly announced enforcement actions against merchants engaging in price manipulation.

    Weights and Measures Fraud Has Its Own Enforcement Track

    Selling goods with inaccurate weight, volume or quantity, such as a scale rigged to overstate weight at a market stall, is treated as a distinct consumer protection violation with its own inspection and penalty framework separate from general pricing complaints.

    Municipal and commerce inspectors periodically test scales and measuring equipment at markets and retail outlets, and consumers who suspect they were shortchanged on quantity can report the specific merchant for investigation.

    Financial Services Complaints Route Through SAMA Instead

    Consumer complaints specifically about banks, insurance companies or finance products go through the Saudi Central Bank's own consumer protection channels rather than the Ministry of Commerce, since SAMA regulates the financial sector directly.

    This division of responsibility means a shopper unhappy with a retail purchase and a customer unhappy with a bank fee use two different regulatory paths, even though both are, broadly speaking, consumer protection issues.

    Telecom Service Complaints Route Through CST

    Disputes over internet, mobile phone billing, or telecom contract terms are handled through the Communications, Space and Technology Commission rather than general consumer protection channels, reflecting the same sector-specific regulatory pattern seen with banking.

    Consumers should identify which regulator oversees the specific sector involved in their complaint, since filing with the wrong agency, while not harmful, can delay resolution compared with going directly to the correct oversight body.

    Real Estate Purchase Disputes Have Their Own Bodies

    Consumer-style complaints about property purchases, particularly off-plan real estate and developer obligations, are generally handled through real estate regulatory bodies and the escrow account framework rather than general Ministry of Commerce consumer channels.

    This is a distinction worth knowing before assuming a general consumer complaint line can help with a stalled property handover, since the applicable protections and remedies differ from those covering a defective retail purchase.

    Written Contracts Strengthen a Consumer's Position

    Whenever a purchase involves a significant amount, a service agreement or a warranty, having the terms in writing, whether a printed contract, an email confirmation or an in-app order summary, materially strengthens a consumer's ability to enforce their rights later.

    Verbal promises made by a salesperson that are not reflected anywhere in writing are far harder to prove and enforce if a dispute arises, so consumers are generally advised to request written confirmation of any non-standard promise before paying.

    Consumers Can Escalate Unresolved Complaints Formally

    If a direct approach to a business fails to resolve an issue, consumers can escalate through the 1900 hotline, the Ministry of Commerce's online complaint portal, or the National Consumer Protection Association, each offering a documented escalation path.

    Serious or unresolved disputes involving significant sums can also be pursued through commercial courts, though many complaints are resolved through the administrative and mediation channels before reaching that stage.

    Foreign Residents and Tourists Have the Same Basic Rights

    Consumer protection law in Saudi Arabia generally applies based on the transaction taking place within the country, not on the buyer's nationality or residency status, meaning tourists and expatriates hold the same basic return, refund and defect rights as citizens.

    This is a practical reassurance for visitors making retail purchases during a trip, since being a short-term visitor does not, in itself, weaken the legal standing to seek a refund or replacement for a defective item bought locally.

    Subscription and Recurring Billing Rules Require Clear Consent

    Businesses offering subscription services or recurring billing must obtain clear, informed consumer consent for the charges and provide a straightforward way to cancel, rather than relying on buried terms or automatically renewing charges without adequate notice.

    Complaints about difficult-to-cancel subscriptions or unexpected recurring charges are a recognized category the Ministry of Commerce addresses, reflecting a broader global trend of regulators scrutinizing subscription business practices more closely.

    Food Safety Complaints Also Fall Under Consumer Protection

    The Saudi Food and Drug Authority works alongside general consumer protection channels to handle complaints about unsafe, spoiled or mislabeled food products, an area with its own inspection regime given the direct health implications involved.

    A consumer who buys spoiled or mislabeled food can typically report it either through the retailer's channel, the general 1900 hotline, or directly to the food authority, depending on how serious or urgent the safety concern appears.

    Service Contracts Have Their Own Cancellation Standards

    Beyond physical goods, contracts for services such as gym memberships, salons, or repair work must clearly state cancellation terms and any early-termination fees, and consumer protection guidance discourages contracts that make cancellation unreasonably difficult.

    A consumer who signs up for a service and later finds cancellation buried behind excessive steps, unresponsive staff, or undisclosed fees has grounds to raise the issue with the ministry as a potentially unfair contract practice.

    Loyalty Points and Gift Cards Carry Their Own Fine Print

    Terms attached to loyalty programs and gift cards, such as expiration dates or usage restrictions, must be disclosed clearly to consumers at the time of issuance, and unreasonably restrictive or hidden terms can themselves become grounds for a consumer complaint.

    Consumers holding a gift card or loyalty balance from a business that later closes or changes ownership should check whether consumer protection guidance addresses their specific situation, since outcomes can vary depending on how the transfer or closure was handled.

    Rental Car and Travel Service Disputes Follow the Same Framework

    Complaints about a rental car company, tour operator or travel service provider operating within Saudi Arabia are generally covered by the same general consumer protection framework as retail goods, including rights around misrepresented services and unfair fees.

    Tourists dealing with an unexpected fee or a service that did not match what was advertised can file a complaint through the same 1900 hotline used for retail issues, since travel services are not carved out as a separate regulatory category.

    Businesses Face Escalating Penalties for Repeat Violations

    The Ministry of Commerce applies escalating consequences for merchants with repeated consumer protection violations, moving from a warning on a first offense toward fines, and eventually toward suspension or closure for persistent bad-faith conduct.

    This graduated approach means a single, isolated customer complaint against an otherwise compliant business typically results in a lighter administrative response than a pattern of complaints showing a systemic problem at that merchant.

    Public Awareness Campaigns Aim to Educate Consumers

    The Ministry of Commerce periodically runs public awareness campaigns explaining consumer rights, common scams, and how to file a complaint, recognizing that many consumers do not exercise rights they are unaware they hold.

    These campaigns often run alongside seasonal shopping peaks, such as before major sales events, when complaint volume related to delivery delays, misleading discounts and defective goods tends to rise noticeably.

    Sale and Clearance Pricing Must Reflect Genuine Discounts

    Regulations require that advertised sale or clearance prices reflect a genuine reduction from a real prior selling price, prohibiting the common deceptive practice of artificially inflating a price shortly before marking it down for a sale event.

    Consumers who suspect a discount is not genuine, for instance because the item was never actually sold at the claimed original price, can report this as a form of deceptive advertising to the Ministry of Commerce for investigation.

    Product Recalls Follow a Coordinated Notification Process

    When a safety defect is identified in a product already sold to consumers, manufacturers and importers are expected to coordinate a recall with relevant authorities, including public notification so affected consumers know to stop using the item and seek a remedy.

    Consumers should register product warranties where possible and watch for official recall announcements from the Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization or the Ministry of Commerce, since recall notices are the primary way affected buyers learn about a safety issue.

    Small Claims Can Move Through Simplified Commercial Courts

    For consumer disputes that cannot be resolved through the ministry's administrative process and involve a defined financial claim, Saudi Arabia's commercial court system, including simplified tracks for smaller claims, provides a formal legal avenue for resolution.

    Pursuing a case through the courts is generally a longer and more involved process than a hotline complaint, so most consumers reserve this route for higher-value disputes or cases where a business has ignored administrative escalation entirely.

    Rules Continue to Evolve With E-Commerce Growth

    As online shopping and social-commerce sales have grown rapidly in Saudi Arabia, the Ministry of Commerce has continued updating implementing regulations and guidance to address new scenarios like livestream selling and marketplace-model platforms.

    Consumers and businesses relying on rules for a specific edge case, such as a purchase made through a social media livestream sale, should check the ministry's current guidance rather than assuming older general e-commerce rules cover every new format.

    Keeping Records Is the Single Best Preparation

    Across nearly every category of consumer complaint, having an order confirmation, receipt, warranty card, and any written communication with the seller substantially improves the speed and outcome of a complaint, whether filed with the business, the NCPA, or the ministry.

    Consumers who habitually save digital or paper records for significant purchases put themselves in a far stronger position if a dispute arises later, compared with relying on memory or a business's own internal records that may not favor the customer.

    FAQ

    What is the toll-free number to file a consumer complaint in Saudi Arabia?

    Dial 1900, the Ministry of Commerce's toll-free hotline, available twenty-four hours a day to report violations or file complaints against a business.

    Can I return an item bought online if I simply change my mind?

    Yes, generally. Under the E-Commerce Law you can return most online purchases within seven days of the contract date without giving a reason, as long as the item is unused, subject to a list of excluded categories.

    What products are excluded from the seven-day online return right?

    Newspapers and magazines, downloaded software, CDs and DVDs, custom-made products, and transportation or catering services are excluded from the standard seven-day return right.

    What happens if my online order is very delayed?

    If a shipment is delayed more than fifteen days beyond the promised delivery window, you can cancel the order and receive a full refund; sellers must also proactively inform you of delays.

    What is the National Consumer Protection Association?

    The NCPA is a government-supported advocacy body offering free advice, mediation between consumers and businesses, and legal referrals, separate from the Ministry of Commerce's own enforcement channel.

    What are my rights if I buy a defective product?

    You are entitled to a replacement or full refund for a defective, adulterated or counterfeit product, free repair if the defect is fixable, and compensation if it cannot be repaired within the warranty period.

    How do I check if an online store is legitimate?

    Look for a Maroof verification badge, which confirms the store is registered with the Ministry of Commerce and has provided verifiable business details.

    Do tourists have consumer protection rights in Saudi Arabia?

    Yes. Consumer protection generally applies based on where the transaction occurs, not the buyer's nationality, so tourists hold the same basic return, refund and defect rights as citizens.

    Where do I complain about a bank or telecom billing issue instead?

    Banking and finance complaints go through SAMA's consumer protection channels, while telecom billing disputes go through CST, not the Ministry of Commerce's general consumer line.

    Can I get a refund if a discount turns out to be fake?

    Yes. Advertised discounts must reflect a genuine reduction from a real prior price. A discount that was never genuine can be reported to the Ministry of Commerce as deceptive advertising.

    What can I do if a business ignores my complaint?

    Escalate through the 1900 hotline, the ministry's online complaint portal, or the NCPA. Serious or high-value disputes can ultimately go to the commercial courts.

    Do I need to keep receipts to file a complaint?

    Yes, it is strongly recommended. Order confirmations, receipts and warranty documentation substantially improve the speed and outcome of any consumer complaint.

    Can I cancel an online order right after placing it by mistake?

    Yes, you generally have twenty-four hours to correct or cancel an order through the store's official channels if you notice a mistake immediately after purchase.

    What penalties can the Ministry of Commerce impose on violating businesses?

    Penalties can include a warning, fines of up to SAR 1 million, temporary or permanent suspension of e-commerce activity, and blocking of the online store, depending on the severity and repetition of the violation.

    Can I cancel a gym membership or service contract easily?

    Service contracts must clearly state cancellation terms and any fees. A contract that makes cancellation unreasonably difficult or hides fees can be reported to the Ministry of Commerce as an unfair practice.
